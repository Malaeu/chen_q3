Subject: A parity question about the negative-time extension of S_t in "On the Hilbert space derived from the Weil distribution"

Dear Professor Suzuki,

I am writing about your paper "On the Hilbert space derived from the Weil distribution" (arXiv:2301.00421v3; Canadian Journal of Mathematics, 2025), which I have been using in a project on Weil positivity around the canonical test.

On page 3, after the definition (1.5) of S_t(z) for nonnegative t, the paper sets, for negative t, S_t(z) := S_{-t}(z) (and, after (3.2), P_t(z) := P_{-t}(z)). With this even extension, S_t^sharp is an even function of t. The transform (1.7) applied to D psi = i psi' then vanishes identically for every even psi in C_c^infinity(R), since psi' is odd. On the other hand, for a narrow even bump psi_L supported in an interval shorter than log 2, the Weil form satisfies <psi_L, psi_L>_W > 0 (indeed at least 2 ||psi_L||^2 for L small; this is the classical short-support positivity). So the equality (1.9) of Theorem 1.4, ||P̂_{D psi}||^2 = pi <psi, psi>_W, cannot hold for such psi with the even extension, independently of the Riemann Hypothesis.

It seems to me that the intended extension is the signed one, P_{-t}(z) = P_t(-z) for t > 0 (equivalently the expansion (3.2) taken for all real t), under which (3.7)-(3.8) hold with the signed exponentials and the argument of Sections 3-4 goes through as written. Could you confirm that this is the intended reading, or point me to what I have misunderstood?

If the signed extension is indeed intended, a short erratum might help readers; I would be glad to be corrected if I am mistaken.

With kind regards,

Eugen Malamutmann
University of Duisburg-Essen
