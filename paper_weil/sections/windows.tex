\section{Finite windows: what the numbers say and what they cannot say}\label{sec:windows}

\begin{figure}[t]\centering\includegraphics[width=.8\linewidth]{figures/fig3_window_bottoms.pdf}
\caption{Bottom eigenvalue $\lambda_1(m,N)$ of the finite window matrix $K(m,N)$ of \cite{CCM-ZST-2025} as a function of the
number of modes $N$, for three windows $m=\lambda^2$. Each curve saturates at $N^*\approx4.6m,\,6.3m,\,8m$; the saturated values are
$3.48\cdot10^{-59}$, $1.8\cdot10^{-112}$, $1.7\cdot10^{-220}$. All entries are interval-arithmetic certified (\texttt{arb}, 220--300 digits).}
\label{fig:bottoms}\end{figure}

\paragraph{Bottoms.} The bottom $\lambda_1(m,N)$ of the CCM window matrix is an upper bound for $\mu_\lambda=\inf\Q$ on the window
(\Cref{sec:setup}). \Cref{fig:bottoms} shows its dependence on the number of prolate modes for three windows; it saturates at
$N^*(m)\approx4.6m,\,6.3m,\,8m$ for $m=13,23,43$, with $N^*/m$ growing. The saturated bottoms agree, within $20\%$, with the
Landau--Widom decay law fitted by Zhu \cite{XUEFENGZHU-2026} on the same object in additive coordinates, and the two-sided window
certificates of that paper match our production cells to $0.03\%$. We record this as a consistency check between two independent
implementations, not as evidence about $\mu_\lambda$ beyond the computed cells: a positive bottom on finitely many windows is an upper
bound for finitely many infima. Positivity of \emph{all} $\mu_\lambda$ is RH.

\paragraph{The prolate trial.} CCM \cite[\S7]{CCM-ZST-2025} propose the prolate packet $k_\lambda$ (with $c=2\pi\lambda^2$, $m=\lambda^2$)
as a trial vector. Its normalised transform is the Mellin transform of $k_\lambda$, and its ratio to $\Xi/\Xi(0)$ is a purely archimedean
factor (the zeta function cancels identically). Define the curvature $\kappa(v)=-\frac{F_v''(0)}{2F_v(0)}$ of the transform $F_v$ of a test $v$,
so that $\kappa_\Xi=\tfrac12(\log\xi)''(\tfrac12)=\tfrac12\bigl[-8+\tfrac14\psi'(\tfrac14)+(\zeta'/\zeta)'(\tfrac12)\bigr]=0.0231049931$.
Expanding the archimedean factor gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trialjet}
 \kappa(k_\lambda)=\kappa_\Xi-\frac{1}{16\pi m}-\frac{13}{256\pi^2m^2}+O(m^{-3}),
\end{equation}
a derivation we have checked against the certified cells $m=13,23,43,83$: the measured $a_m=m(\kappa_\Xi-\kappa(k_\lambda))$ equals
$0.020307,\,0.020123,\,0.020016,\,0.019957$, and the fit $a_\infty+b/m$ returns $a_\infty=0.019892$ against $1/(16\pi)=0.019894$ and
$b=0.005143$ against $13/(256\pi^2)=0.005145$ (\Cref{fig:jet}). On the saturated windows the bottom vector coincides with the trial
to $p=1-\langle\xi_1,k_\lambda\rangle^2\approx5\cdot10^{-9}$ ($m=13$) and $5\cdot10^{-10}$ ($m=23$); the curvature difference
$\kappa(\xi_1)-\kappa(k_\lambda)$ on the production schedule $N=m$ is $0.38\,T_m$ with $T_m=\frac{L^2}{4\pi^2}\sum_{k>N}k^{-2}$,
$L=\log m$, across all six cells $m=13,\dots,313$.

\paragraph{What the wide windows cannot give.} It is tempting to read the saturation as a mechanism: if the bottom vector converges
to the trial with bounded relative error $\eps_\infty=\|\xi_1-k_\lambda\|/\sqrt{\lambda_1}$ on the wide schedule ($\eps_\infty=1.35,1.33$
at $m=13,23$), one would like to conclude $\mu_\lambda>0$ along the sequence. But $\mu_\lambda>0$ cofinally implies $\mu_\lambda\ge0$ for all
$\lambda$ by the monotonicity of \cite{CCM-ZST-2025}, which is RH. Likewise the weaker-looking statement $\lambda_1\ge-o(\lambda_2)$ is
RH-equivalent, because the second level is bounded above by the min--max on a fixed two-dimensional test space. Every premise of this
kind is the conclusion in disguise, and the numbers of this section are diagnostics for the location of the difficulty, not steps toward
its removal.
