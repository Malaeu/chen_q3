Positivity of $\Phi$ on $[0,\infty)$: $h(u)<0$ only for $u<\sqrt{3/(2\pi)}=0.691$, so for $x\ge0$ every term $h(ne^x)$ of \eqref{eq:Phi} is positive;
for $x<0$ use evenness. Hence $\Phi>0$ everywhere.

Envelope constants. Writing $\Phi(x)=4e^{x/2}\sum_n p_0(ne^x\cdot ne^x)\ldots$ is cumbersome; instead put $P_0(y)=4y^2-6y$ so that
$4e^{x/2}h(\sqrt y)=e^{x/2}P_0(\pi y)e^{-\pi y}/\ldots$; the clean statement is that $\Phi^{(j)}(x)$ is a finite sum of terms $e^{x/2}P_j(\pi n^2e^{2x})e^{-\pi n^2e^{2x}}$
with $P_0(y)=4y^2-6y$ and $P_{j+1}(y)=\tfrac12P_j(y)+2y\bigl(P_j'(y)-P_j(y)\bigr)$, so $P_1(y)=-8y^3+30y^2-15y$. Bounding each monomial by its maximum
against half of the Gaussian, $|y^re^{-y/2}|\le(2r/e)^r$, and summing the $n\ge2$ terms geometrically gives
$|\Phi^{(j)}(x)|\le AM_j\,e^{-\frac\pi2e^{2|x|}}$ with $M_j=\frac{\pi^{-1/4}}{A(1-e^{-3\pi/2})}\sum_r|P_{j,r}|\bigl(2(r+\tfrac14)/e\bigr)^{r+1/4}$, i.e.\
$M_0=23.910$, $M_1=325.43$.
