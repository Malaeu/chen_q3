# Atlas Card #1 — Modularity for the Prime Comb: What Plays the Role Modular Forms Played for E8?

> Status: Track B strategy/review material. This is not a proof of RH, not a
> Lean proof file, and not a Step33, L3, or `Q3.Main` route mutation.
>
> Verification note (2026-06-12): the main 2020-2026 claims were spot-checked
> against primary or near-primary sources. One attribution was corrected:
> the failed de Branges-type positivity condition is Conrey--Li
> (`arXiv:math/9812166`), not Sarnak.

## TL;DR

- **There is no single known “magic” structure that does for the prime side of the Weil explicit formula what modular forms did for E8. The closest functional analogue is the Connes–Consani–Moscovici prolate/Sonin/Toeplitz structure (the Carathéodory–Fejér mechanism), which is the only candidate that natively encodes the two-sided positivity-plus-sign constraints your “edge defect on a cone of cross-correlations” needs; the Bondarenko–Radchenko–Seip zeta-zero interpolation basis is the closest structural analogue of CKMRV interpolation but is conditional and built for the zero side, not the prime side.**
- **A hard negative result frames the whole program: the prime/zero explicit-formula measure is provably NOT a Fourier quasicrystal (Kurasov–Sarnak, even under RH), so the Lee–Yang / crystalline-measure machinery cannot be transplanted directly; and Conrey–Li proved a natural de Branges positivity certificate for RH actually fails. No one has ever built a Cohn–Elkies-style single dual certificate for zero location/RH — only for proportions of zeros, prime gaps, and first-zero heights.**
- **Recommended ranking of candidate “modularities”: (1) Connes–Consani prolate/Toeplitz–Carathéodory–Fejér structure [unconditional positivity at one place + small support; directly about your quadratic form]; (2) Carneiro–Littmann–Vaaler Beurling–Selberg/de Branges extremal-function technology [unconditional, already runs on the explicit formula]; (3) Bondarenko–Radchenko–Seip interpolation at zeta zeros [conditional, dual side]; (4) Fourier quasicrystals / Lee–Yang [structurally appealing but obstructed by the not-a-FQ theorem]; (5) direct automorphic/Eisenstein inputs [no transfer mechanism identified].**

## Key Findings

1. **The E8 analogy is exact only at the level of the LP/positivity skeleton, not the special-function input.** In Cohn–Elkies the entire problem reduces to ONE magic function with prescribed signs of f and f̂; Viazovska built it from quasimodular/weakly holomorphic forms, and CKMRV’s universal optimality used Fourier interpolation bases at √(2n) nodes built from modular forms. The Weil-positivity problem has the same skeleton (reduce RH to positivity of one quadratic form on a constrained cone), but the object that must be controlled — the prime comb Σ Λ(n)n^{−1/2} δ_{log n} — is provably less structured than a lattice theta series.
1. **The prime comb is NOT a Fourier quasicrystal (THEOREM, Kurasov–Sarnak 2020).** This is the single most important structural fact for the program: it rules out a naive “modularity for primes” via crystalline-measure machinery.
1. **The Connes–Consani–Moscovici line provides the closest genuine structural analogue** — a self-adjoint (prolate) operator on the Sonin space whose compression makes archimedean Weil positivity manifest, plus a Carathéodory–Fejér/Toeplitz mechanism (Connes–van Suijlekom 2025) that forces zeros onto the critical line, validated numerically with strikingly few primes (Connes–Consani–Moscovici 2025; Connes 2026).
1. **The Carneiro–Littmann–Vaaler extremal-function technology already runs on the explicit formula unconditionally** and is the most directly transferable toolkit for your edge-strip defect, because it is designed precisely to control sums over primes/zeros by band-limited majorant/minorant pairs with sign constraints.
1. **Weil positivity is unconditionally known for small support (THEOREM, Yoshida; reproved Bombieri 2000; Connes–Consani 2021).** This pins down where your “hard regime” begins and gives the natural benchmark for the ratchet induction.

## Details

### Section 1 — Direct attempts at Viazovska-style dual certificates / LP / magic functions for zeta

**Cohn–Elkies LP and Viazovska’s magic function (background; THEOREM).** Cohn–Elkies, *New upper bounds on sphere packings I*, Ann. of Math. 157 (2003) 689–714 (arXiv:math/0110009), reduce the packing density bound to a single radial f with f(0)=f̂(0)>0, f(x)≤0 for |x|≥r, f̂≥0;   the bound is sharp only in d=1,2,8,24. Viazovska (Ann. of Math. 185 (2017) 991–1015; arXiv:1603.04246) built the d=8 magic function from weakly holomorphic/quasimodular forms (Eisenstein E₂,E₄,E₆ and a weight-3/2 form); Cohn–Kumar–Miller–Radchenko–Viazovska, *Universal optimality of E8 and Leech* (Ann. of Math. 196 (2022) 983–1082; arXiv:1902.05438), upgraded this to interpolation with derivatives. The bound is known NOT to be sharp in dimensions 3–5, 6 (“Six-dimensional sphere packing and linear programming”, arXiv:2211.09044), 12, and 16 — i.e. magic functions are believed to exist only in extraordinarily symmetric dimensions.

**The +1 / −1 sign-uncertainty bridge (THEOREM).** Bourgain–Clozel–Kahane (*Principe d’Heisenberg et fonctions positives*, Ann. Inst. Fourier 60 (2010) 1215–1232) introduced the sign-uncertainty quantity A₊(d). Gonçalves–Oliveira e Silva–Steinerberger, *Hermite polynomials, linear flows on the torus, and an uncertainty principle for roots* (J. Math. Anal. Appl. 451 (2017) 678–711; arXiv:1602.03366), proved a self-dual extremizer exists with r(f)=r(f̂)=A₊(d): in their words, “the optimal c satisfies 0.41 ≤ c ≤ 0.64 … We improve the one-dimensional result to 0.45 ≤ c ≤ 0.594 … We also prove that extremizers exist, and have infinitely many double roots” (THEOREM, unconditional). Cohn–Gonçalves, *An optimal uncertainty principle in twelve dimensions via modular forms* (Invent. Math. 217 (2019) 799–831; arXiv:1712.04438), proved A₊(12) = √2  by a Viazovska-style modular magic function and explicitly identified the sign uncertainty principle with the Cohn–Elkies LP bound — the cleanest example of “one magic function decides a sign-constrained extremal problem.” The original BCK motivation was real zeros of Dedekind zeta functions of number fields,  the only place sign uncertainty touches zeta directly.

**Carneiro–Littmann–Vaaler / Beurling–Selberg line applied to zeta (THEOREM / CONDITIONAL).** Carneiro–Littmann–Vaaler, *Gaussian subordination for the Beurling–Selberg extremal problem* (Trans. AMS 365 (2013) 3493–3534), is the engine; it produces band-limited majorant/minorant pairs whose Fourier transforms are explicit, designed to plug into the Guinand–Weil explicit formula. Applications: Carneiro–Chandee–Milinovich bounding S(t), S₁(t) (Math. Ann. 356 (2013)); Carneiro–Chirre–Milinovich, *Bandlimited approximations and estimates for the Riemann zeta-function* (Publ. Mat. 63 (2019) 601–661; arXiv:1710.10362); Carneiro–Chandee–Littmann–Milinovich, *Hilbert spaces and the pair correlation of zeros* (J. Reine Angew. Math. 725 (2017) 143–182; arXiv:1406.5462), which casts pair-correlation extremal problems in reproducing-kernel/de Branges Hilbert spaces. These are CONDITIONAL on RH where they bound S(t)/zero counts, but the extremal-function constructions themselves are unconditional THEOREMS.

**Fourier optimization → prime gaps (CONDITIONAL on RH).** Carneiro–Milinovich–Soundararajan, *Fourier optimization and prime gaps* (Comment. Math. Helv. 94 (2019) 533–568; arXiv:1708.04122), solve a one-sided sign-constrained Fourier extremal problem C⁺(A)=sup_F [F(0) − A∫_{|t|≥1}(F̂)₊ dt]/‖F‖₁ via the explicit formula. Their key constant is C⁺(36/11) > 1.1943, giving the conditional statement that “for all x > 2, there is always a prime number between x and x + (22/25)√x log x” (later sharpened to 0.8358√x log x by Carneiro–Chirre et al., arXiv:2005.02393). This is the explicit formula used as a tool, not as a positivity certificate.

**Pair-correlation via semidefinite programming (CONDITIONAL).** Chirre–Gonçalves–de Laat, *Pair correlation estimates for the zeros of the zeta function via semidefinite programming* (Adv. Math. 361 (2020) 106926; arXiv:1810.08843), replace bandlimited auxiliary functions “by the class of functions used in the linear programming bounds developed by Cohn and Elkies for the sphere packing problem” and improve proportions of simple/distinct zeros and small-gap counts. Crucially this bounds PROPORTIONS of zeros, not their LOCATION; further work: Chirre–Quesada-Herrera (Fourier optimization and quadratic forms, Q. J. Math. 2022), Carneiro–Milinovich–Quesada-Herrera–Ramos (Fourier optimization, least quadratic non-residue, arXiv:2404.08380).

**NEGATIVE SEARCH (the central question).** No paper constructs a Viazovska-style single extremal “magic function” / dual LP certificate that proves, even conditionally, the location of zeta zeros on the critical line, or that certifies Weil’s quadratic-form positivity by exhibiting one extremizer. The LP/SDP/sign-uncertainty technology has been transplanted to zeta only for (a) prime gaps conditional on RH, (b) proportions/counts/gaps of zeros (pair correlation), and (c) first-zero heights / number-field discriminants. The structural reason: Weil positivity is a ∀-quantified positivity of an infinite quadratic form over a convolution algebra involving infinitely many primes, not a finite-support LP admitting a single dual certificate. Conrey–Li (*A note on some positivity conditions related to zeta- and L-functions*, arXiv:math/9812166, 1998) proved a natural de Branges-type positivity condition for RH actually fails: “It is possible that these positivity conditions are too strong for Hilbert spaces of entire functions associated with the Riemann zeta function.”

### Section 2 — The Fourier interpolation wave after Radchenko–Viazovska

**Radchenko–Viazovska 2019 (THEOREM).** *Fourier interpolation on the real line* (Publ. Math. IHÉS 129 (2019) 51–81; arXiv:1701.00265): every even Schwartz f is recovered from {f(√n), f̂(√n)}_{n≥0}; the interpolation basis is an integral transform of weakly holomorphic modular forms of weight 3/2 for the theta group Γθ. This is the prototype “reconstruction from values at √n nodes.”

**Bondarenko–Radchenko–Seip 2020/2023 (CONDITIONAL / THEOREM depending on statement).** *Fourier interpolation with zeros of zeta and L-functions* (Constr. Approx. 57 (2023) 405–461; arXiv:2005.02996). They construct Fourier interpolation bases for functions analytic in a strip;  the headline example recovers f̂ from the values of f at ±log(n)/4π and of f̂ at the points (ρ−1/2)/i with ρ ranging over nontrivial zeros of ζ. They establish a duality principle via Dirichlet-series kernels with meromorphic continuation and a functional equation, and a strengthening of Knopp’s abundance principle. Absent RH the nodes can be off the real line and the formula converges only after grouping terms. They explicitly note (§2.5) that these bases generate “crystalline” measures, tying back to Guinand, Kurasov–Sarnak, Lev–Olevskii, Meyer. This IS the closest structural analogue of CKMRV interpolation for the zeta setting — but it reconstructs from values at zeta ZEROS (the spectral side), not from data on the prime comb, and the unconditionality of the basis depends on facts equivalent to or stronger than RH for the cleanest form.

**Uniqueness pairs and the critical threshold (THEOREM).** Kulikov–Nazarov–Sodin, *Fourier uniqueness and non-uniqueness pairs* (J. Math. Phys. Anal. Geom. 21 (2025) 84–130; arXiv:2306.14013), give nearly matching sufficient conditions for discrete (A,B) to be Fourier uniqueness/non-uniqueness pairs via Wirtinger–Poincaré-type inequalities; the Radchenko–Viazovska √n pair sits exactly at the supercritical/critical boundary. Ramos–Sousa, *Fourier uniqueness pairs of powers of integers* (J. Eur. Math. Soc., arXiv:1910.04276) and *Perturbed interpolation formulae and applications* (arXiv:2005.10337), show the formulas can be perturbed; Ramos–Stoller (arXiv:2103.12015) extend to higher dimensions; Bondarenko–Radchenko–Seip–Sun, *The basis functions of Fourier interpolation* (arXiv:2512.18677), give precise zero-distribution estimates and surprising non-uniqueness “excess.” Kulikov, *Fourier interpolation and time-frequency localization* (arXiv:2005.12836), uses the BRS setting to recover the Riemann–von Mangoldt zero-counting law up to a log² error term — direct evidence that the interpolation framework “sees” the density of zeta zeros.

**Assessment.** Together this gives “reconstruction of admissible functions from their values at zeta zeros” — a genuine CKMRV analogue. But the data live on the ZEROS, so it is the natural language for the spectral (A(h)) side, not the prime (P(h)) side; transferring it to control the edge-strip prime defect requires a duality flip not yet carried out in the literature.

### Section 3 — Fourier quasicrystals / crystalline measures as candidate “modularity for primes”

**Kurasov–Sarnak (THEOREM + key NEGATIVE result).** *Stable polynomials and crystalline measures* (J. Math. Phys. 61 (2020) 083501; arXiv:2004.05678) construct positive crystalline measures / Fourier quasicrystals from torus zero-sets of multivariate Lee–Yang (stable) polynomials, answering Meyer’s question. **Decisive for your program**, they write verbatim: “Various examples of crystalline measures that are not Dirac combs were constructed by Guinand. Note however that his example 4 page 264 coming from the explicit formula in the theory of primes does not give a Fourier quasicrystal, even assuming the Riemann hypothesis.” So the prime/zero comb is crystalline-like (a summation duality) but FAILS the temperedness of |μ|, |μ̂| that defines a Fourier quasicrystal. Olevskii–Ulanovskii (2020) and Alon–Cohen–Vinzant, *Every real-rooted exponential polynomial is the restriction of a Lee–Yang polynomial* (arXiv:2303.03201), complete the classification of ℕ-valued FQs; Alon–Vinzant (Rev. Mat. Iberoam. 40 (2024); arXiv:2307.13498) give gap distributions; Alon–Kummer–Kurasov–Vinzant (arXiv:2407.11184) extend to higher dimensions; Favorov (Anal. Math. 50 (2024) 455–462; arXiv:2401.01121) exhibits a crystalline measure that is not an FQ.

**Explicit formula as Poisson-type summation (THEOREM, qualitative).** Meyer, *Measures with locally finite support and spectrum* (PNAS 113 (2016) 3152–3158), states the Riemann–Weil explicit formula has the form μ vs. σ with Dirac masses on zeros vs. masses on log-primes, but “the measures μ studied by André Weil in 1952 are NOT crystalline measures” because of the integral term ω (from the Γ-factor) and the requirement of exponential decay on the test function. Baake–Spindeler–Strungaru and the survey *Measures, modular forms, and summation formulas of Poisson type* (Comm. Math. Phys. 2025; arXiv:2405.15620) place Guinand’s distribution, Cohn–Gonçalves, Lev–Reti formulas in a common framework whose coefficients involve modular forms. So the explicit formula is a Poisson-summation ANALOGUE with an unavoidable archimedean (continuous, log|x| conductor) defect — exactly the term that breaks crystallinity.

**Assessment.** The crystalline-measure program identifies precisely WHY there is no clean “modularity for primes”: the archimedean place contributes a continuous/non-discrete term and the masses Λ(n)/√n grow, so the prime comb is neither a Fourier quasicrystal nor a generalized Dirac comb. Lee–Yang positivity is therefore a structural ANALOGY (real-rootedness ↔ critical line) rather than a transfer tool.

### Section 4 — Summation formulas replacing modularity: trace formulas, prolate/Sonin, spectral triples

This is the richest vein and the strongest candidate.

**Connes–Consani, Weil positivity at the archimedean place (THEOREM, conditional framing).** *Weil positivity and trace formula, the archimedean place* (Selecta Math. 27 (2021) no. 4, Paper 77; arXiv:2006.13771). The root of positivity is the trace of the scaling action compressed onto the orthogonal complement of the cutoff projections (Λ=1); the difference between the Weil distribution and the Sonin trace is expressed via prolate spheroidal wave functions and controlled with Hermitian Toeplitz matrices. They strengthen Yoshida’s theorem and give an operator-theoretic reason for positivity at the single archimedean place; “all the ingredients make sense in the general semilocal case, where Weil positivity implies RH.”

**Prolate operator ↔ zeta (THEOREM/numerical).** Connes–Moscovici, *The UV prolate spectrum matches the zeros of zeta* (PNAS 119 (2022) no. 22; arXiv:2112.05500): the self-adjoint extension of the prolate operator restricted to the complement of the interval has negative eigenvalues whose UV behavior reproduces the squares of zeta zeros, with eigenfunctions in the Sonin space — tying to the Connes–Consani positivity proof. Connes–Consani–Moscovici, *Zeta zeros and prolate wave operators* (Ann. Funct. Anal. 15 (2024) 87; arXiv:2310.18423) integrate this into the semilocal trace formula and prove stability of the semilocal Sonin space under increasing the finite set of places.

**Spectral triples / ζ-cycles and the 2025–2026 results (THEOREM + numerical).** Connes–Consani, *Spectral triples and ζ-cycles* (Enseign. Math. 69 (2023) 93–148; arXiv:2106.01715) exhibit very small eigenvalues of the Weil quadratic form restricted to small support; eigenvectors come from prolate functions; the low-lying spectrum of a conditioned spectral triple resembles low zeta zeros. The positivity is delicately sensitive to the prime p=2: verbatim, “The value of the smallest eigenvalue of σ₊ for μ=3 is < 6×10⁻⁸ … one finds that the smallest eigenvalue λ(p) for L=log 3 is negative for p=1.9999 and also for p=2.0005, so that the positivity requirement restricts the choice of p to an interval of size < 10⁻³ around p=2.” Connes–van Suijlekom, *Quadratic forms, real zeros and echoes of the spectral action* (Comm. Math. Phys. 2025; arXiv:2511.23257): a C*-algebraic Carathéodory–Fejér mechanism — a positive-semidefinite Toeplitz matrix of rank n−1 has its kernel polynomial’s zeros on the unit circle — extended to convolution operators, forcing zeros of the eigenfunction’s Fourier transform onto the real line. Connes–Consani–Moscovici, *Zeta spectral triples* (arXiv:2511.22755): rank-one perturbations of the scaling-operator spectral triple on [λ⁻¹,λ], using only Euler factors over primes p ≤ x = λ², produce operators whose spectra match the lowest zeta zeros with striking accuracy; a rigorous proof of convergence would establish RH. Connes’ 2026 survey, *The Riemann Hypothesis: Past, Present and a Letter Through Time* (arXiv:2602.04022): verbatim, “Using only primes less than 13, this optimization procedure yields approximations to the first 50 zeros with accuracies ranging from 2.6×10⁻⁵⁵ to 10⁻³. Moreover we prove a general result that these approximating values lie exactly on the critical line ℜ(z)=1” (he adds the likelihood of chance agreement is “about 10⁻¹²³⁵”). The on-the-line statement is a THEOREM via the Carathéodory–Fejér/Hurwitz argument; the matching to true zeta zeros is HEURISTIC/numerical.

**Assessment.** The trace-formula side ALREADY supplies the special structure — but on the spectral/archimedean side, via the prolate/Sonin self-adjoint operator and the Toeplitz Carathéodory–Fejér positivity mechanism. This is the genuine functional analogue of “modular forms control the lattice sum”: a self-adjoint operator + positivity structure controls the explicit formula’s archimedean term and forces zeros onto the line. What it does NOT yet do is control the prime side at large support unconditionally — exactly your edge-strip defect.

### Section 5 — Obstructions and negative results

- **Prime comb is not an FQ (Kurasov–Sarnak, THEOREM):** rules out direct Lee–Yang transfer.
- **Weil/Guinand explicit-formula measure is not crystalline (Meyer, THEOREM):** the archimedean integral term ω obstructs it.
- **de Branges positivity certificate for RH fails (Conrey–Li, math/9812166, THEOREM/negative):** “these positivity conditions are too strong.”
- **Sign-uncertainty lower bounds (BCK; GOSS; Cohn–Gonçalves, THEOREM):** A₊(d)>0 strictly bounds how concentrated a sign-constrained f and f̂ can simultaneously be — any magic function for a two-sided sign problem pays an irreducible “uncertainty tax,” and Cohn–Dong–Gonçalves (*Sign uncertainty principles and low-degree polynomials*, arXiv:2210.01684) show low-degree polynomial×Gaussian ansätze cannot beat degree 3 asymptotically.
- **Weil positivity is unconditionally TRUE for small support (THEOREM):** Yoshida (*On Hermitian forms attached to zeta functions*, 1992); reproved with the minimum attained in the unit ball of L²[−t,t] and positive-definite for t small by Bombieri, *Remarks on Weil’s quadratic functional in the theory of prime numbers, I* (Atti Accad. Naz. Lincei, 2000) and *The Riemann Hypothesis* (Clay Millennium, 2006); given an operator-theoretic reason by Connes–Consani 2021. The hard regime begins when the support is large enough that the prime terms with log n in the edge strip [≈2K,4K] enter and can drive the smallest eigenvalue negative — precisely the Connes–Consani p=2 sensitivity at scale L≈log 3 quoted above.

### Section 6 — Synthesis: candidate structures vs. the edge-strip prime defect on a cone of cross-correlations

For each candidate: what it preserves / drops on transfer; unconditionality; one concrete finite experiment.

**(a) Beurling–Selberg / Carneiro–Littmann–Vaaler extremal functions.**

- Preserves: explicit band-limited majorant/minorant pairs with controlled Fourier support and sign — exactly matched to ker-Q boundary conditions and to a short prime sum. Drops: it controls one-sided (majorant/minorant) error, not the genuine two-sided ±sign cone of cross-correlations; loses sharp constants once you demand both f and f̂ constraints.
- Inputs unconditional: YES (Gaussian subordination is a theorem).
- Experiment: on a window log n ∈ [2K,4K] with K small (say K = ½ log 7), numerically build the CLV extremal majorant/minorant of the indicator of the edge strip, evaluate the resulting bound on the prime-side defect, and compare to the true defect computed from primes p ≤ e^{4K}; check whether the extremizer’s Fourier transform shape matches the ker-Q-compatible cone.

**(b) Bondarenko–Radchenko–Seip interpolation at zeta zeros.**

- Preserves: a genuine reconstruction/duality basis tied to the zero set and a functional equation; the CKMRV analogue. Drops: lives on zeros (spectral side), needs RH for the clean real-node form; does not natively produce prime-side coefficients.
- Inputs unconditional: PARTIALLY (basis exists unconditionally; clean √-node-style statement is conditional).
- Experiment: take the BRS basis truncated to the first N zeros, expand a ker-Q test function h⋆h̃, and measure how the prime-side P(h) is represented in the basis coefficients; test whether truncation error in the edge strip decays as the Riemann–von Mangoldt density predicts (Kulikov’s log² law).

**(c) Fourier quasicrystals / Lee–Yang (Kurasov–Sarnak).**

- Preserves: the real-rootedness ↔ critical-line analogy and a constructive route to crystalline summation duals. Drops: EVERYTHING quantitative for the prime comb, since that comb is provably not an FQ; only an analogy survives.
- Inputs unconditional: YES but inapplicable to the prime comb directly.
- Experiment: approximate the truncated prime comb (primes p ≤ P) by the FQ of a Lee–Yang polynomial fit to the same low-frequency data, and measure the obstruction (the growth of |μ| and the archimedean ω term) as P grows — quantify exactly how non-FQ the comb is, validating the Kurasov–Sarnak statement numerically and bounding the defect.

**(d) Trace formulas / Connes–Consani prolate–Sonin–Toeplitz.**

- Preserves: a self-adjoint operator + Carathéodory–Fejér positivity that natively makes the archimedean Weil term positive AND forces zeros onto the line — the deepest structural match to your Q(h) ≥ 0 on Hermitian squares. Drops: the prime side enters only through finite Euler factors; large-support / many-prime positivity is conjectural; the matching of perturbed-spectral-triple spectra to zeros is numerical, not proven.
- Inputs unconditional: archimedean positivity + small support YES; the prime-side control (your edge defect) is exactly the open part.
- Experiment: reproduce the Connes–Consani ζ-cycle eigenvalue computation at L = 2 log λ spanning the edge strip [2K,4K], track the smallest eigenvalue of the even/odd Weil quadratic-form matrices σ± as primes up to e^{4K} are switched on one at a time, and test whether the prolate-derived eigenvectors stay ker-Q-compatible — i.e., whether the “ratchet” survives each new prime.

**(e) Direct automorphic / Eisenstein inputs (spectral interpretation à la Zagier).**

- Preserves: the conceptual source of modularity (Eisenstein series gave Viazovska’s E₂/E₄/E₆ and the optimality obstruction E₆). Drops: no identified mechanism converts an Eisenstein/automorphic input into a prime-side certificate for the explicit formula; the Selberg trace formula is an analogue, not a tool that controls Λ(n)/√n.
- Inputs unconditional: N/A — no transfer mechanism exists in the literature (NEGATIVE SEARCH).
- Experiment: as a falsification test, attempt to fit the numerically computed edge-strip extremizer (from experiment (d)) against low-weight Eisenstein-series Fourier coefficients; a null result quantifies that the automorphic route adds nothing beyond the prolate structure.

## Recommendations

1. **Stage 1 (now): build on the Connes–Consani prolate/Toeplitz structure as the primary candidate.** It is the only one that natively encodes both (i) unconditional archimedean Weil positivity and (ii) a Carathéodory–Fejér mechanism putting zeros on the line. Run experiment (d): reproduce the ζ-cycle smallest-eigenvalue computation, extend the support into your edge strip [2K,4K], and switch primes on one at a time. **Benchmark that changes the plan:** if the smallest eigenvalue of σ± stays bounded below by a positive constant as each edge-strip prime enters (uniformly in the ker-Q cone), the ratchet is structurally supported and you should formalize the prolate eigenvector as your “magic” object. If it dips negative (as the p=2 sensitivity at L≈log 3, smallest eigenvalue < 6×10⁻⁸ within a window of width < 10⁻³ around p=2, warns), the prolate structure alone is insufficient and you must add the CLV majorant control of candidate (a).
1. **Stage 2 (parallel): use Carneiro–Littmann–Vaaler extremal functions to get an unconditional, explicit upper bound on the edge defect** (experiment (a)). This is the lowest-risk path to your “single open analytic lemma” because the inputs are unconditional theorems. **Threshold:** if the CLV bound on the edge defect is smaller than the archimedean positivity margin from Stage 1 across the whole ker-Q cone, the lemma is proved.
1. **Stage 3 (structural insurance): treat Bondarenko–Radchenko–Seip as the dual-side bookkeeping device**, not as the certificate generator (experiment (b)). Use it to confirm that your reconstruction error tracks the Riemann–von Mangoldt density; if it does not, the cone is mis-specified.
1. **Do NOT invest in Lee–Yang/FQ transfer or direct automorphic transfer as load-bearing**: the not-a-FQ theorem and the absence of any transfer mechanism make these analogies, not tools. Use them only as falsification/quantification experiments (c), (e).

## Caveats

- The Connes–Consani–Moscovici and Connes–van Suijlekom matching of perturbed-spectral-triple spectra to zeta zeros is NUMERICAL; the convergence that would yield RH is explicitly unproven. The 2026 “Letter Through Time” 50-zeros-from-primes-below-13 result is a striking computation (with the on-the-critical-line property proved via Carathéodory–Fejér/Hurwitz), not a theorem about RH itself.
- “Weil positivity for small support is unconditional” (Yoshida/Bombieri), “the prime comb is not an FQ” (Kurasov–Sarnak), and “de Branges positivity for RH fails” (Conrey–Li) are the three firm theorems that bound the program; everything connecting modularity to the prime side is currently analogy or numerics.
- The Bondarenko–Radchenko–Seip clean interpolation statement and several “reconstruction from zeta zeros” claims are CONDITIONAL on RH (node reality); absent RH, convergence requires term-grouping.
- No literature constructs a magic function/dual certificate for zero location; this is a genuine gap, not an oversight — the ∀-quantified infinite-quadratic-form nature of Weil positivity is the obstruction.
