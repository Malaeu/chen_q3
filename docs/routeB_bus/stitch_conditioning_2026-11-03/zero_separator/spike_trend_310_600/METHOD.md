The complete κ scan enumerates event sectors(γ_j/Ω,γ_(j+1)/Ω],Ω=2πm/logm. A sector is rejected numerically if λ_r+g<−λ_0−g or λ_r+g<0, by singular-value minmax. Otherwise its gap is bounded above by λ_r because δ≥0. This permits pruning after a positive incumbent. When λ_r itself reaches the float guard, unresolved sub-resolution sectors remain explicitly UNRESOLVED_TINY_SEPARATOR rather than inferred to fail. Evaluated gaps and pruned upper bounds form global numerical max-gap brackets. The guard is max(inherited matrix radius,1000eps64 d max(1,||A||)); it is a heuristic numerical budget, not an outward operator-error certificate. Typical global gap brackets are a few1e−9 wide. Thus16 unresolved ζ cells do not prove exact nonexistence or exact zero gaps.

There are two distinct optimizations: δ_max(m) is the maximum residual overκ, attained atκ1 by nested quiet spaces; g*(m) is the maximum gap overκ. Its optimizing sector need not have maximalδ. SUMMARY.csv retains both. Control δ_max values were computed independently, not copied from the gap optimizer. BINS.csv groups the30 m values into six fixed contiguous five-sample blocks. Median gap means median of the five best-per-m gaps, NOT median overκ sectors. For descriptive medians and plots only, unresolved gaps are represented by0, which lies within their retained lower/upper brackets. No meaningful median-gap slope is fit to controls whose maxima are all at that floor.

FITS.csv reports OLS, R², Theil–Sen and leave-one-out slope ranges. These are exploratory descriptions of a deterministic sparse finite grid; there are no probability or asymptotic confidence claims. Weak residual R² and the sign-changing leave-one-block-out median slope preclude a reliable rate extrapolation. Exact closure, source positivity, a cofinal separator or RH do not follow.
