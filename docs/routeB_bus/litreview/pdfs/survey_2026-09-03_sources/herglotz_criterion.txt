    A scalar Herglotz criterion for the even-simplicity hypothesis
                in the localized Weil quadratic form

                                                 Abstract
          The strategy of Connes–Consani–Moscovici (arXiv:2511.22755) and Connes–van Suijlekom
      (arXiv:2511.23257) toward the Riemann Hypothesis rests on a structural hypothesis on the
      truncated/localized Weil quadratic form QW : that its smallest eigenvalue be simple with even
      eigenvector (even-simplicity, CCM Def. 5.3). In those works even-simplicity is assumed and
      verified only numerically; Suzuki (arXiv:2606.09096) proves it for the continuum operator only
      in the small-interval limit. We give an exact reduction of this hypothesis to a single scalar
      inequality between two Herglotz functions. Writing the pole term of the explicit formula as
      the rank-two operator W0,2 = 2|C⟩⟨C| − 2|S⟩⟨S| (C(x) = cosh x2 even, S(x) = sinh x2 odd),
      the parity decomposition turns each sector into a rank-one perturbation of the pole-free
      operator: Aeven = Be + 2|C⟩⟨C| on the even sector and Aodd = Bo − 2|S⟩⟨S| on the odd
      sector, where Be has a single negative eigenvalue (the Perron ground of the pole-free part)
      and Bo > 0. The two sector ground states are the smallest roots of the rank-one secular
      equations me (λ) := ⟨C, (Be − λ)−1 C⟩ = − 21 and mo (λ) := ⟨S, (Bo − λ)−1 S⟩ = + 12 . Our main
      result (Theorem 4) is that even-simplicity is equivalent to the pole-localization condition

      make no claim of proving even-simplicity itself, and no claim about the Riemann Hypothesis.


1    Introduction
The Connes–Consani–Moscovici (CCM) program [1] constructs, from the Weil quadratic form
QNW λ of the explicit formula, self-adjoint operators whose spectra approximate the nontrivial
zeros of ζ. A pivotal ingredient, made precise in the companion paper of Connes–van Suijlekom
[2], is the following structural property of the truncated form.
Definition 1 ([1], Def. 5.3). A real symmetric operator T commuting with a Z/2-grading γ
is even-simple if its smallest eigenvalue is simple and the corresponding eigenvector ξ satisfies
γξ = ξ.
    The CvS theorem is conditional: if the bottom of the spectrum is a simple eigenvalue
with even eigenvector, then the Fourier–Mellin zeros of that eigenvector lie on the critical line.
Even-simplicity is therefore the analytic hypothesis on which the spectral realization rests. In
[1] it is “assumed simple”

6    Relation to the CCM hypothesis and outlook
Theorem 4 reduces the even-simplicity hypothesis of [1, 2] — assumed there and verified only
numerically — to pole-localization plus the single scalar inequality (6). The reduction is exact
and uses only the parity decomposition, the rank-one structure of the pole term, and the Herglotz
character of the two sector resolvent forms. It replaces the nodal/positivity formulation (the
ground state of A being one-signed), which requires the full operator to be positivity-improving

                                                  4
and therefore holds only for small a (Appendix A), by a statement that does not: the criterion is
about the odd-sector resolvent form mo evaluated at the even ground energy, and is meaningful
for every a.
    The remaining task is to prove (6) for all c (equivalently all a). This is a uniform bound
on a single Herglotz resolvent form at an energy λeven
                                                   0   → 0+ , and is the precise residual of the
CCM strategy at this step. The Herglotz/Weyl m-function framework that produces (6) is the
same one used to control the parity-separated prolate spectral determinants in [6]; pursuing the
uniform bound within that framework is the natural next step. We make no claim of proving (6),
even-simplicity, or the Riemann Hypothesis.


A      Why the nodal/positivity route is not the right formulation
A natural attempt proves even-simplicity by showing the ground state of A is nodeless (one-
signed), whence even (a nodeless eigenfunction of a parity-commuting operator is even). This is
Suzuki’s route [3], valid for small a via positivity improving. It does not extend, and we record
why, since the obstructions motivate the sector formulation of the main text.

    1. A is not positivity-improving for large a. The smooth off-diagonal kernel is −g ′′ (t) =
                    e−|t|/2                           ∗                                ∗
       2 cosh 2t − 1−e −2|t| , which changes sign at t = 0.281199 . . . [4]; for 2a > t it is not sign-

       definite, so A has no positivity-improving semigroup. Directly, Aeven = Be + 2|C⟩⟨C| adds
       the positive off-diagonal kernel 2C(x)C(y) > 0 to the operator, breaking the sign condition;
       the resolvent (Aeven − µ)−1 has negative entries for all µ.

    2. The cross-energy/inertia route is self-defeating. Writing the even ground φ0 = (Be −
       λeven
        0    )−1 C and assuming it changes sign, the index-one inertia of Be − λeven
                                                                                0    forces the total
                                                                +
       nodal cross-energy to satisfy δtot ≤ α(2α + 1) (α = ⟨C, φ0 ⟩); every form-positivity estimate
       (Cauchy–Schwarz, ground-state representation, Picone) gives this same upper direction,
       while a contradiction would need the reverse lower bound, which is generically false (the
       upper bound is strict).

    3. Reflection positivity does not apply. It yields a ground state in a positive cone (nodelessness),
       not in the even parity sector, and the relevant reflection acts on a doubled Hilbert space;
       moreover the pole kernel 2 cosh x−y 2 is rank-two indefinite.

The sector formulation of §2–4 bypasses all three: it never asks for one-signedness, only for the
ordering of two sector bottoms, which the rank-one Herglotz criterion (6) captures exactly.
