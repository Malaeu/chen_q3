                                              ASPECTS OF THE SCREW FUNCTION CORRESPONDING TO
                                                         THE RIEMANN ZETA-FUNCTION

                                                                                  MASATOSHI SUZUKI


                                                  Abstract. We introduce a screw function corresponding to the Riemann zeta-function
                                                  and study its properties from various aspects. Typical results are several equivalent
                                                  conditions for the Riemann hypothesis in terms of the screw function. One of them
                                                  can be considered an analog of so-called Weil’s positivity or Li’s criterion. In addition,
arXiv:2206.03682v4 [math.NT] 30 May 2023




                                                  we prove a few partial but unconditional results for such equivalents.

   The functions in class Ga have various fruitful connections with many different mathe-
matical objects, for example, due to a relation with positive-definite functions described
in [16]. Therefore, more interesting discoveries and connections are expected for the
screw function of the Riemann zeta-function from the origin mentioned above. Further-
more, although this paper focused only on the Riemann zeta-function for simplicity, it
would be natural to extend the results of this paper to other general L-functions like
automorphic L-functions or L-functions in the Selberg class. However, we leave such
studies for other papers [22, 25, 26, 27, 28] and future research.

   This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we prove Theorem 1.1 and a lemma
needed in later sections. In Section 3, we prove Theorem 1.3 after reviewing the Weil
distribution and preparing for the notation. In Section 4, we establish the pointwise non-
negativity of Ψ(t), and the non-negativity of Gg (t, u) for small t, u, as a preliminary step
towards proving Theorem 1.4. Moreover, we state and prove a lower bound for hφ, φiGg ,a
under restrictions to φ. In Section 5, we prove Theorem 1.4 using results in Section 4
and make a comparison with Yoshida’s results. In Section 6, we prove Theorem 1.5 and
study eigenvalues of the kernel Gg (t, u). In Section 7, we prove Theorems 1.6 and 1.7,
and 1.8. In Section 8, we describe explicit relations between the moments µn and the
Li coefficients. In Section 9, we describe a Kreı̆n string corresponding to Qξ (z) in (1.7)
under the RH. In Section 10, we discuss the mean-periodicity of the screw function g(t).
In Section 11, we introduce variants Ψω (t) of Ψ(t) and state an analog of Theorem 1.7.
Further, we study relations among different Ψω (t)’s.

2.4. Lemma. The following lemma is often used in subsequent sections.
Lemma 2.1. Let F (z) be an entire function of the exponential type and let A be a
complex number. Suppose that F (γ) = A for all zeros γ of ξ(1/2 − iz). Then F (z) = A
as a function.
Proof. It suffices to deal only with case of A = 0. The number of zeros of F (z) in the
disc |z| ≤ r counted with multiplicity is O(r) by the assumption. On the other hand,
the number of distinct zeros of ξ(1/2 − z) in the disc |z| ≤ r is not O(r) by [29, Section
9.12], which was first proved by Littlewood. This is a contradiction if F 6= 0.          

   The known result that the number of simple zeros of ζ(s) on the critical line up to
height T is bounded below by T log T is more convenient for the proof of Lemma 2.1
(see Conrey [6] and his comments on the result of Levinson (1974) in the introduction).

                                5. Proof of Theorem 1.4
   First, we show the second half of Theorem 1.4. Since g(t) is continuous and hermitian
on the real line, the integral operator Gg [a] on L2 (−a, a) defined in (1.12) is a self-adjoint
Hilbert–Schmidt operator for every 0 < a < ∞. Therefore, the spectrum of Gg [a] consists
of eigenvalues and zero. Whether or not zero is an eigenvalue is not determined by the
general theory. The hermitian form h·, ·iGg ,a is non-negative definite if and only if all
eigenvalues of Gg [a] are non-negative, and the nondegeneracy for h·, ·iGg ,a is equivalent
to that Gg [a] does not have zero as an eigenvalue, since hφ1 , φ2 iGg ,a = hGg [a]φ1 , φ2 iL2
and there exists an orthonormal basis for L2 (−a, a) consisting of eigenfunctions of Gg [a].
5.1. Proof of necessity. If the RH is true, Gg (t, u) is non-negative definite on (−a, a)
for every 0 < a < ∞ by Theorem 1.2. Therefore, all eigenvalues Gg [a] are non-negative
([23, §8]). If there exists an eigenfunction for eigenvalue zero, it must be zero as a
function by (3.1), (3.2), and Lemma 2.1. This is a contradiction. Hence, h·, ·iGg ,a is
nondegenerate.                                                                         
