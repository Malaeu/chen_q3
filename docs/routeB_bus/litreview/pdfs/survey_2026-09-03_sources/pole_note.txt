                                          Abstract
     Let Aa be the self-adjoint operator on L2 (−a, a) associated with the localized Weil
quadratic form QaW of Connes–Consani–Moscovici (arXiv:2511.22755) and Suzuki (arXiv:2606.09096),
realized through the screw function g of the Riemann zeta function. The missing analytic
step in the CCM strategy toward RH — as stated by Connes (arXiv:2602.04022, §6.6) — is
that the smallest eigenvalue of the (truncated) Weil form be simple with even eigenfunction.
Suzuki proved this for the continuum operator for sufficiently small a; we first locate the
obstruction to extending the classical positivity argument: the smooth off-diagonal kernel
of Aa is −g ′′ (t) = 2 cosh(t/2) − e−t/2 /(1 − e−2t ), which violates the Beurling–Deny sign
condition precisely for |t| > t∗ where t∗ = 0.28119957 . . ., so the classical route is confined to
a ≤ t∗ /2 ≈ 0.1406.
     The main observation of this note is that the violating term 2 cosh x−y      2   is exactly the
pole term W0,2 of the explicit formula — an explicit rank-two operator (we verify the
kernel identity to 10−301 ) — and that it splits by parity as +2 |C⟩⟨C| on the even sector
(C(x) = cosh x2 > 0) and −2 |S⟩⟨S| on the odd sector (S(x) = sinh x2 ). Consequently the
pole-free part A ea := Aa − W0,2 (in CCM notation: −WR − P Wp , the archimedean-plus-
                                                                      p
primes part) has nonpositive off-diagonal kernel for all a > 0: the sign barrier was entirely
produced by the poles of ζ. An explicit form identity (Proposition 3, verified to machine
precision) exhibits A  ea as a positive jump Dirichlet form plus a lower-bounded potential;
by Beurling–Deny, irreducibility and Perron–Frobenius this yields, with no restriction on
a, a simple, strictly positive and even ground state for A    ea (Theorem 1). This removes the
“sufficiently small a” restriction under which the same conclusion was obtained for the full
Aa in [4].

Theorem 1 (Unconditional Perron structure for the pole-free part). For every a > 0 the lowest
eigenvalue of Aea is simple, and its eigenfunction can be chosen strictly positive a.e. on (−a, a);
in particular it is even.

Proof. Since the kernel 2 cosh x−y2 of R is bounded on the compact interval, R is a bounded (indeed
Hilbert–Schmidt) self-adjoint operator; hence A    ea = Aa − R is self-adjoint, lower semibounded,
with the same form domain as the operator Aa constructed in [4, Thm. 1.1], [1, Thm. 3.6], and
with compact resolvent. We argue in four steps.
     (1)R Aea is a Dirichlet form plus a lower-bounded potential. By Proposition 3, ⟨A     ea v, v⟩ =
E(v)+ κa |v|2 , where E is the symmetric pure-jump form with strictly positive kernel J(x−y) > 0

Remark 1. The difficulty is structural, not a matter of estimate size: λ0 lies above the single
                       eeven
negative eigenvalue of Aa    and exponentially close to its deep positive ladder (the near-resonances
are the prolate structure of [1, §8]), so φ0 is a hyper-fine cancellation across the whole spectrum
rather than a perturbation of any single mode. Concretely, the naive split of −g ′′ at t∗ produces a
sign-violating part whose norm equals the spectral gap of the sign-good part to within a percent
at every cutoff (∥K+ ∥/γ0 → 1.01 for c = 2, . . . , 53); the bottom of the Weil form is critically
tuned, consistent with its arithmetic meaning. This is why no soft norm bound decides the sign
and the nodal/oscillation route is the appropriate one.
Remark 2. Suzuki’s unconditional Theorem 1.5 [4] and the CCM determinant theory [1, Thm. 5.10]
both concern reality of zeros of associated entire functions; the present reduction addresses the
complementary hypothesis (even-simplicity) those results either bypass or assume. The splitting
also isolates why the problem is hard: the poles of ζ, not the primes, carry the entire obstruction
to classical positivity.

Data and code availability
All computations use closed-form archimedean entries validated against the defining integrals,
with the Hurwitz–Lerch function evaluated by its defining series and, near the singular endpoint,
by the small-parameter expansion in Suzuki; both regimes are cross-validated against each other
and against the defining integrals (the library defect documented in mpmath issue #1099 and
DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20671635 makes independent validation essential in exactly the parameter
range required here). Scripts available from the author.
