    A Loewner/operator-monotone framework for the even-simplicity
       problem in the Connes–Consani–Moscovici spectral triple
                                              Breno Andrade

                                               June 17, 2026


                                                  Abstract
          The Connes–Consani–Moscovici “Zeta Spectral Triples” program (arXiv:2511.22755) uses
      the hypothesis (Def. 5.3, Thm. 5.10) that the lowest spectral value of the truncated Weil
      quadratic form is simple and has an even eigenvector (“even-simplicity”). We observe that
      the truncated Weil matrix is, verbatim, the Loewner (divided-difference) matrix of an odd func-
      tion ψ(k) = k h(k 2 ), and that its parity sectors are themselves Loewner matrices of the functions
      h and Φ(ξ) = ξh(ξ) in the variable ξ = k 2 (the sector identities are algebraic, and were confirmed
      numerically to 10−121 in the Galerkin implementation). This gives a precise Loewner/operator-
      monotone framework for the even-simplicity problem. In the generic case where h is globally
      operator monotone, Loewner’s theorem and the Bhatia–Sano conditional-definiteness theorem
      imply even ground-state ordering. The arithmetic Weil matrices lie instead in a doubly-critical
      finite-node regime; that is kept as the remaining open core. No claim toward the Riemann
      Hypothesis, nor a proof of the arithmetic even-simplicity hypothesis, is made.

3    A generic operator-monotone ordering theorem
By Löwner’s theorem, Lh ⪰ 0 (for all nodes) iff h is operator monotone on (0, ∞); and then Φ = ξh
is operator convex with Φ(0) = 0 (Bhatia, Matrix Analysis Thm. V.2.9; Bhatia–Sano, Math. Ann.
344 (2009)). For a fixed truncation, Proposition 1 identifies positivity of the odd sector with
positivity of the finite Loewner matrix Lh on the nodes {12 , . . . , N 2 }. If this finite-node positivity
holds for all node sets, Loewner’s theorem identifies it with global operator monotonicity of h. The
next theorem treats this global, generic regime.

Remark 1 (the arithmetic case is not covered). Theorem 1 requires global operator monotonicity of

the positive weighting of the odd. This doubly-critical regime is the even-simplicity hypothesis itself
(CCM, Def. 5.3), which Connes–Consani–Moscovici, and Suzuki (for small a only), leave open.
    It is useful to separate three layers. Positivity of either sector is equivalent to global positivity
of the corresponding Loewner matrix, hence to operator monotonicity of h (respectively of Φ);
by Yoshida’s criterion odd positivity already implies the Riemann Hypothesis, so establishing the
global Pick property of the arithmetic h is not a shortcut but a reformulation of that problem. The
even ordering for operator-monotone h is, by contrast, settled by Theorem 1, in the regime where
the even sector is indefinite. The remaining target is Conjecture 1: the ordering λeven   0    < λodd
                                                                                                  0    in
the doubly-critical regime, which is arithmetic-dependent — the prime term brings both Loewner
matrices to the positive-semidefinite boundary, and without it the ground state is odd — and which,
by Proposition 2, is the explicit scalar inequality bT (A − λo I)−1 b > h(0) − λo . This is the natural
object to study independently of the full Riemann Hypothesis; possible non-variational routes are
de Branges / Krein-string asymptotics of the two critical near-null eigenvalues in Suzuki’s screw-
function realisation (Appendix A), the analysis of the signed spectral density of ψ (negative prime
atoms together with the positive pole/archimedean continuum over ω ∈ (0, 1)) and the parity of
the explicit-formula cancellation, and the secular relation δe < δo between the two sector gaps,
observed numerically but not yet proved.
    Finally, the contribution of the present note is the bridge itself: the CCM, CvS and Bombieri
formulations of even-simplicity work in the Weil-form basis and make no contact with operator-
monotone functions, whereas the object is in fact a Loewner matrix and the hypothesis an operator-
monotone ordering, which brings the Löwner–Donoghue–Bhatia–Sano theory to bear.
