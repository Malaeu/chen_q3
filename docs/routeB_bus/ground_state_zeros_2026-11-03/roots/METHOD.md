# Fourier-zero census method and pre-run prediction

The primary object is the literal even CCM eigenvector supplied by the spectral worker, with L=log(m), N=m. Odd vectors are also supported when the full ground belongs to the odd block. A solver-unresolved vector must never be promoted to an actual ground by this census. The vectors are consumed as decimal coefficients in the phased orthonormal basis; no source zero is used to seed or choose a Fourier root.

Put Ω=2πm/L and u=(z/Ω)². In even parity,

F(z)=2sin(zL/2)/(sqrt(L)z) P(u)/D(u),
D(u)=Π[n=1..m](u−n²/m²),
P(u)=c0D(u)+sqrt(2)Σ[n=1..m]cn u D(u)/(u−n²/m²).

P has degree at most m. In odd parity the numerator is proportional to Σ[n=1..m]dn n D(u)/(u−n²/m²), with degree at most m−1; there is an additional forced origin zero. The physical odd transform is imaginary on the real axis, which does not change its zero locations.

Every internal harmonic pole is removed by the sine prefactor. The harmonic is a physical zero only when the corresponding coefficient vanishes; a root of P at that harmonic remains a valid Fourier zero. All exterior harmonics n(2π/L), n>m, are exact Fourier zeros of every vector in this finite carrier and must be included independently below the cutoff. They are labeled `forced_external` and never presented as recovered source structure. Pure imaginary roots are separately counted, with one positive imaginary representative for each negative-real u root. Generic conjugate u roots yield two positive-real-height Fourier roots.

Flint ball arithmetic constructs P at adaptive precision, then isolates its complete complex root set; degree accounting is mandatory. The coefficient enclosures have exactly zero imaginary parts and Flint returns a complete collection of disjoint unique-root enclosures. These prerequisites permit conjugation classification of real roots: an axis-intersecting disk is called real only if its reflected enclosure overlaps its own disk and no other isolated disk. Otherwise precision escalates. Root balls enclose roots for the supplied decimal-vector polynomial. They do not enclose errors in the exact functional, matrix, or eigenvector. The rational Fourier sum evaluates the physical transform at every root, with stable sinc continuation at harmonics. An independent sinc sample (approximately thirteen polynomial roots per vector) checks the continuation and rational identity. This residual checks algebra and continuation; it is not a substitute for isolation and is not used to match source zeros.

The primary cutoff is κΩ, κ=1+(1+log log m)/(4log m). Ω and 0.8Ω are controls. Matching includes physical roots with 0<Re(z)<T and |Im(z)|<1/2, and source points in the same strip. It minimizes total Euclidean distance one-to-one. Outputs retain all matched pairs, raw complex errors, local source spacing, and spacing-scaled errors; source/fourier count surplus is separately retained. Threshold counts at 1e−2, 1e−6 and 1e−12 qualify numerical closeness. A matched pair need not be close, and no tolerance-free assignment is called a recovered source root. Source DH points use z=γ+i(1/2−σ); each upper-half-plane off-line pair contributes two positive-height roots. The planted pair is z=2π/log2±i/4. Zeta reference input has approximately 160 digits, so smaller reported deviations encounter that reference floor.

Frozen prediction before receiving ground vectors: forced exterior zeros will contribute a substantial, explicitly identifiable part of the primary edge census, and edge behavior can differ from the 0.8Ω interior control. A planted negative mode and the DH off-line modes may appear differently in even and odd grounds. We do not presuppose that the ground Fourier zeros equal the source census, or that small Fourier values at source ordinates imply nearby roots. The experiment will compare isolated roots and multiplicities directly.

`controls.py` manufactures single-mode even/odd transforms and a mixed polynomial with 13 positive-real, one negative-real and two nonreal u roots. `controls.json` records passing degree, location and direct-transform checks. These controls exercise the root census independently of any spectral claim.

## Ground-vector precision replay

Same-vector arithmetic replay only checks polynomial construction. Independently rebuilt low/high source matrices and eigenvectors provide the separate physical precision check. At m=16,24,40,80,160 the low numerator is also completely isolated. The completed zeta m=300 pilot additionally has a complete low numerator census. Bulk cells and DH/planted m=300 replay every high root in the primary physical strip, plus bounded pure imaginary roots, by rational Newton on the independently rebuilt low vector. These seeds are isolated high-vector roots; no source ordinates are used. Convergence and duplicate checks are retained. Such low Newton counts are explicitly replayed-root counts and are not completeness certificates for the low polynomial. Failures remain failures and are excluded from precision-qualified scaling fits.

For each polynomial root, the physical Fourier derivative is evaluated analytically. On the real axis at a fixed carrier L, the observed vector infinity difference gives the coefficient-perturbation Cauchy-Schwarz diagnostic sqrt(L)*sqrt(dim)*delta_v. Off the real axis this requires the growth factor specified in `PRECISION_SCOPE_ERRATUM.md`; frozen raw unscaled off-axis diagnostics are superseded there. Its ratio to |F'| is a pessimistic first-order shift diagnostic, not an error enclosure for the exact ground. At m160 it is very large near the edge, while actual independent-vector root replay is stable; both diagnostics are retained separately. Measured replay shifts include a conservative nominal allowance for the 1e-165 u isolation target and 180-digit output. Differences below this numerical resolution are not reported as resolved hundreds-of-digit stability.

Input bytes and their hash are frozen when a root job starts, avoiding a metadata race if a streaming spectral vector is replaced while processing. Each root artifact binds its supplied vector and reference input. The aggregate table also binds the final exact reference-file bytes. A terminal-newline provenance repair changed no parsed DH values, and requires no root recomputation.

## Additional m300 low-vector interval check

`low_bracket_validation_m300.json` independently validates all roots of the five relevant LOW decimal-vector numerators at m300 (zeta even; DH and planted even and winning odd). Arb at 2000 bits proves opposite rational-function signs at the recorded exact 205-digit decimal endpoints. Exact rational comparisons exclude every pole from each bracket and prove pairwise disjointness. The leading coefficient is proved nonzero. The number of disjoint real-u brackets equals the polynomial degree, so every rounded LOW numerator root is enclosed and simple. For negative u, the associated Fourier roots are purely imaginary. The single DH even pure-imaginary root beyond the primary cutoff is validated in a separate bounded supplemental check. These checks took approximately thirteen seconds plus the short supplement; no global far-root search was performed.

The LOW interval check applies only to the frozen decimal eigenvector model. It does not turn empirical source reconstruction, spectral precision replay, or matrix residual diagnostics into a certificate for the exact ground or the original source function.

## Reviewed zeta-even continuation producer

After the original complete complex-isolation runs, a bounded pilot and an independent review validated a faster zeta-even path. It uses only the adjacent completed m−1 ground-vector polynomial roots as seeds, rational MPFR Newton continuation, and at most four Vieta/Newton-power-sum missing-root seeds. It then independently proves strict opposite Arb signs on exact decimal, pairwise disjoint, pole-free real-u brackets, a nonzero leading coefficient, and degree-many roots. Every output root ball contains its checked bracket and has radius less than 1e−165 (the implementation targets 1e−167). These final interval/degree gates establish completeness; real roots are not assumed from the seeds. No source ordinates are used. Any failed gate or other exception falls back to the full complex Flint census. DH and planted cells keep the original full complex method.

The independent review re-evaluated 396 m190/m206 brackets through separately assembled polynomials at at least 3000 bits and tested invalid input guards and the caller's exception fallback. `continuation_adoption.json` binds the exact old/new code, frozen helper and integration diff, and the review. New artifacts retain their producer hash, method, interval certificate and any fallback reason. Completed current-input artifacts are left immutable. This changes neither source/eigenvector arithmetic nor the experiment's mathematical status.

To reproduce the complete m300 LOW interval artifact, run `reproduce_low_brackets_m300.py`. It runs the base sign checks, the sole DH pure-imaginary supplement, and exact rational endpoint/pole/disjointness checks in staging; only a full five-polynomial PASS is published.
