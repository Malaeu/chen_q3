## 3. A compensation theorem for all three offsets

This section is new source-specific mathematics. No data or trace identity is used.

For x in I put
\[
 b_I(x)=\int_{\ell/2+x}^\infty A(t)dt+
                 \int_{\ell/2-x}^\infty A(t)dt,
\quad \beta_0=2\int_{\ell/2}^\infty A-c_A,
\]
\[
 \Omega(x)=b_I(x)-b_I(0),\qquad
 V_\partial(x)=-\tfrac12\log(1-4x^2/\ell^2).              \tag{18}
\]
Both are nonnegative; their logarithmic endpoint singularities are integrable against the required smooth tests.

**Lemma 1 (endpoint gain).** On I,
\[
 \Omega(x)\ge\frac9{10}V_\partial(x).                     \tag{19}
\]
**Proof.** For `0<t<=ell<d=13/125`, differentiation gives
\[
 -A'(t)=\tfrac12A(t)+\frac{e^{-t/2}}{2\sinh^2t}
 \ge\frac{(1-d/2)(1-d^2)^2}{2t^2}
 >\frac9{20t^2}.
\]
Use `sinh t<=t/(1-t^2)` and `e^(-t/2)>=1-t/2`. Both `Omega` and `V_partial` vanish with zero derivative at the midpoint; moreover
`Omega''=-A'(ell/2+x)-A'(ell/2-x)` and
`V_partial''=[(ell/2+x)^(-2)+(ell/2-x)^(-2)]/2`.
Integrate the second-derivative inequality twice, and use evenness. QED.

**Lemma 2 (weighted overlap).** Let `0<q<ell/4`, `d_0=ell-q`, and suppose `ell/(4q)>=e/2`. On the overlap of the two endpoint strips,
\[
 \sqrt{V_\partial(x)V_\partial(x-d_0)}
 \ge\frac12\log\frac\ell{2q}.                            \tag{20}
\]
**Proof.** Write the distances to the two relevant endpoints as `s` and `q-s`, with `0<s<q`. The two potentials are bounded below by
`(1/2)log(ell/(4s))` and `(1/2)log(ell/(4(q-s)))`.
For `A_0=ell/(4q)` and `t=s/q`, the product
`log(A_0/t) log(A_0/(1-t))` is minimized at `t=1/2` when `2A_0>=e`.
To see this, set `g(t)=t log(A_0/t)` and
`D(t)=g(1-t)-g(t)`. On `t<1/2`,
`D'(t)=2-2log A_0+log(t(1-t))<=2-2log(2A_0)<=0`, and `D(1/2)=0`.
The derivative of the product has sign `-D(t)`. Symmetry proves the minimum. QED.

Consequently a shift pairing is bounded by the two **weighted** strip norms, divided by `(9/20)log(ell/(2q))`, using (19) and Cauchy--Schwarz.

**Theorem 3 (all three offsets are paid).** For arbitrary six complex profiles,
\[
 \boxed{|\langle h,\mathbb O h\rangle|
       \le\sum_p\int_I\Omega(x)|h_p(x)|^2dx.}             \tag{21}
\]
No mean or pole conditions are needed for (21).

**Proof.** Exact integer comparisons give
\[
 q_+<\ell/16,\qquad q_-<\ell/7.                           \tag{22}
\]
For the two edges at u, combine the 2- and 5-profiles into
`k=(w_5 h_2+w_2 h_5)/s_+`, where `s_+=sqrt(w_2^2+w_5^2)<9/10`.
The weighted norm of k on the relevant strip is at most the sum of the two weighted squared norms. Equations (19)--(20) give a denominator at least
`(9/20)log 8>9/10`, since `log2>2/3`.
Therefore `2s_+|<k,T_u h_11>|` is at most the corresponding sum of weighted strip masses, using `2ab<=a^2+b^2`.
For the other edge the denominator is at least
`(9/20)log(7/2)>9/20`, whereas `w_4<7/20`.
The two strips in h_11 are disjoint, because `q_++q_-<ell`; the other profiles occur on distinct coordinates. Summing pays each strip mass at most once. This proves (21). QED.

This is stronger and differently scoped than “the offsets changed one minimum only slightly.” The offsets may be large on a localized profile, but that profile incurs enough endpoint energy. All coefficients in this payment come from the original A and the exact displacements.
