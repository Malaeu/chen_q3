## Conventions and a positive control domain

Use the **logarithmic transport**
\[
x=\log u,\quad d^*u=du/u=dx,\quad g(x)=f(e^x),
\qquad \mathcal Fg(z)=\int_{\mathbb R}g(x)e^{-izx}\,dx.
\]
It preserves the L² norm, multiplication of the group becomes addition, and
\(g^*(x)=\overline{g(-x)}\). The inverse transform on the real line has the factor \(1/(2\pi)\). Inner products and the Weil pairing are antilinear in the first argument. These are [C, (3.5), (3.10)] conventions, not the additive \(2\pi\)-Fourier convention used in the Gaussian calculation below.

Write
\[
\begin{split}
H(g)&=\|g\|_2^2,\qquad C_g(t)=\Re\int\overline{g(x)}g(x+t)\,dx,\\
D_g(t)&=\|g(\cdot+t)-g\|_2^2=2H(g)-2C_g(t),\\
a(t)&=\frac{e^{-t/2}}{1-e^{-2t}},\qquad
\mathcal D(g)=\int_0^\infty a(t)D_g(t)\,dt,\\
A_\pm(g)&=\int g(x)e^{\pm x/2}\,dx,\\
c_A&=\gamma+\log(8\pi)+\pi/2,\qquad d_A=c_A-4,\\
w_n&=\Lambda(n)/\sqrt n.
\end{split}
\]
Here **\(\Lambda\)** is the von Mangoldt function; prime powers retain their full weights. The geometric form is
\[
Q(g)=\mathcal D(g)-c_AH(g)
 +2\Re\bigl(A_+(g)\overline{A_-(g)}\bigr)
 -2\sum_{n\ge2}w_n C_g(\log n).                 \tag{Q}
\]
For compact support the sum is finite. Substitution in [C, (3.7)–(3.11)] gives (Q): the archimedean constant is
\[
\log(4\pi)+\gamma+2\int_0^\infty
 (1-e^{-t/2})a(t)dt=c_A,
\]
since the last integral is \(\tfrac12\log2+\tfrac\pi4\). No component has been discarded.

### Domain lemma [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

Define the **control space**, independently of the sign of Q,
\[
X=\{g:\ \|e^{|x|}g\|_2^2+\mathcal D(g)<\infty\},
\qquad \|g\|_X^2=\|e^{|x|}g\|_2^2+\mathcal D(g).
\tag{X}
\]
Functions are identified almost everywhere. Its inner product is the sum of the weighted L² inner product and the polarized translation energy. Q and its Hermitian pairing B are continuous on X, with the explicit bound
\[
|B(g,h)|\le C_X\|g\|_X\|h\|_X,
\qquad C_X=|c_A|+14.                           \tag{CONT}
\]
Moreover \(C_c^\infty(\mathbb R;\mathbb C)\) is dense in X. On each fixed compact window, X has precisely the translation-energy domain of the local form (Q); the exponential weight there is bounded above and below. We do not posit a globally lower-bounded Weil operator on all of L².

**Proof.** Put \(W_g=\|e^{|x|}g\|_2\) and
\(R_{g,h}(t)=\int\overline{g(x)}h(x+t)dx\). The inequality
\(|x|+|x+t|\ge|t|\) and Cauchy–Schwarz give
\[
|R_{g,h}(t)|\le e^{-|t|}W_gW_h.
\]
Thus the prime pairing is bounded by
\(2W_gW_h\sum_{n\ge2}\log n/n^{3/2}\le10W_gW_h\).
For the last bound, the summand is decreasing for real \(n\ge2\), its first value is below 1, and \(\int_1^\infty\log x\,x^{-3/2}dx=4\).
Also
\[
|A_\pm(g)|\le\sqrt{4/3}\,W_g,
\]
because \(\int e^{\pm x-2|x|}dx=4/3\). The two pole pairings therefore cost at most \(8W_gW_h/3\). Cauchy–Schwarz in the translation-energy integral bounds its pairing by \(\sqrt{\mathcal D(g)\mathcal D(h)}\). Together with the diagonal term these imply (CONT).

For density choose real smooth cutoffs \(0\le\chi_R\le1\), equal to 1 on \([-R,R]\), zero off \([-R-1,R+1]\), with uniformly bounded derivatives. Weighted L² convergence of \(\chi_Rg\) follows by dominated convergence. For the energy of \((1-\chi_R)g\), expand its translated difference into
\[
(1-\chi_R(x+t))(g(x+t)-g(x))
 +(\chi_R(x)-\chi_R(x+t))g(x).
\]
The first term tends to zero and is dominated by the integrable translation energy. For the second use the squared majorant
\(\min(C^2t^2,4)|g(x)|^2\).
Its integral against \(a(t)dt\,dx\) is finite, since \(a(t)=O(1/t)\) at zero and decays at infinity. Dominated convergence proves convergence in X.

For a compactly supported element of X, convolve with a smooth nonnegative compactly supported approximate identity. L² convergence gives weighted L² convergence on the common compact support. For each t the translated difference converges in L², is bounded by twice the original translated-difference norm, and dominated convergence in \(a(t)dt\) gives energy convergence. This proves density. Completeness, when needed, follows by realizing X as the closed graph of \(g\mapsto\sqrt{a(t)}(g(x+t)-g(x))\) from weighted L² to the product L² space. □

## L1. The canonical identity, with its actual normalization and domain

### L1a. Canonical construction [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

Define the standard completed function by analytic continuation of
\[
\xi(s)=\tfrac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s),
\qquad \Xi(z)=\xi(1/2+iz).
\]
Let the printed source of [C, (7.1)] be
\[
h(u)=(\pi^2u^4-\tfrac32\pi u^2)e^{-\pi u^2},
\quad E(h)(u)=u^{1/2}\sum_{n\ge1}h(nu),
\]
and define
\[
\Phi(x)=4E(h)(e^x),\quad A=\|\Phi\|_2>0,\quad f_0=\Phi/A.
\tag{CAN}
\]
Then \(\Phi\) is smooth, strictly positive, even and rapidly decreasing faster than every exponential, as are all its derivatives. Precisely,
\[
\mathcal F\Phi(z)=\Xi(z),\qquad
\|f_0\|_2=1,\qquad \mathcal F f_0(z)=\Xi(z)/A.       \tag{FT}
\]
Thus \(f_0\in X\). On the multiplicative group the corresponding unit test is \(u\mapsto f_0(\log u)\), not a compactly supported test.

**Proof and source-scale check.** For the additive Gaussian transform with kernel \(e^{-2\pi iuy}\), differentiation of the Gaussian identity gives
\[
\widehat{u^2e^{-\pi u^2}}=(1/(2\pi)-y^2)e^{-\pi y^2},
\]
\[
\widehat{u^4e^{-\pi u^2}}
=(y^4-3y^2/\pi+3/(4\pi^2))e^{-\pi y^2}.
\]
Therefore \(\widehat h=h\) and \(h(0)=\int h=0\). Poisson summation, with the zero term removed and the positive and negative terms paired, gives \(E(h)(u)=E(h)(u^{-1})\). This proves evenness in x. Termwise differentiation converges on every compact set. For \(x\ge0\), every term in \(\Phi(x)\) is positive, since \(\pi n^2e^{2x}>3/2\); evenness proves strict positivity everywhere.

For \(\Re s>1\), absolute convergence permits a Mellin calculation:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^\infty h(u)u^{s-1}du
&=\tfrac12\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)
 \bigl[(s/2)(s/2+1)-\tfrac32(s/2)\bigr]\\
&=\tfrac18s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2).
\end{aligned}
\]
Multiplying by \(\zeta(s)\) shows that the Mellin transform of the printed \(E(h)\) is \(\xi(s)/4\), not \(\xi(s)\). With \(s=1/2-iz\), analytic continuation and the functional equation yield (FT). The factor 4 repairs a literal scale discrepancy in the printed formulas; it does not alter their source line or any normalized autocorrelation. It is not fitted to numerical data.

Here is a completely explicit decay envelope, also used in L4. Let
\[
p_0(z)=4z^2-6z,\qquad
p_{j+1}(z)=\tfrac12p_j(z)+2z(p_j'(z)-p_j(z)),
\quad p_j(z)=\sum_r p_{jr}z^r.
\]
For \(x\ge0\), differentiation gives
\(\Phi^{(j)}(x)=e^{x/2}\sum_{n\ge1}p_j(\pi n^2e^{2x})e^{-\pi n^2e^{2x}}\).
Since \(\sup_{z\ge0}z^q e^{-z/2}=(2q/e)^q\) for \(q>0\), and \(n^2\ge1+3(n-1)\),
\[
|f_0^{(j)}(x)|\le A_j e^{-a e^{2|x|}},\quad a=\pi/2,
\tag{ENV}
\]
where the finite, source-defined constants are
\[
A_j=\frac{\pi^{-1/4}}{A(1-e^{-3\pi/2})}
 \sum_r |p_{jr}|\left(\frac{2(r+1/4)}e\right)^{r+1/4}.
\]
Evenness handles negative x. These estimates imply the asserted regularity, all weighted integrability, and \(f_0\in X\). They also justify that the transform in (FT) is entire. □

### L1b. Zero value and the integral identity [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

For every sufficiently rapidly decaying smooth test in the Weil class, the published explicit formula [W, §2.1.1] gives, with multiplicities,
\[
B(g,h)=\sum_{z\in Z(\Xi)}
 \overline{\mathcal Fg(\bar z)}\,\mathcal Fh(z).
\tag{EF}
\]
The sign of the Fourier convention is reconciled by reindexing the zero set using \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\). No location of a zero on the critical line is assumed. The sum has the meaning prescribed in the explicit formula; no interchange of a conditionally convergent sum is needed below, because each canonical summand is exactly zero.

For \(g=h=f_0\), and also for \(g=f_0\), \(h\) compact smooth, the correlation entering that formula is in its Weil class: (ENV) gives smoothness and decay faster than every exponential on the logarithmic line. Under \(u=e^x\), multiplication by \(u^{-1/2}\) gives the two power-decay conditions in [W, §2.1.1]. Thus the use of the noncompact test is licensed by the published formula itself, not by an unproved extension of the compact test class.

The function \(\Xi\) is real entire, so
\(\overline{\mathcal F f_0(\bar z)}=\Xi(z)/A=0\) at every zero in (EF). Consequently
\[
Q(f_0)=0.                                           \tag{NULL}
\]

Put
\[
k(t)=a(t)-e^{-t/2}=\frac{e^{-5t/2}}{1-e^{-2t}},\quad
\mathcal J(g)=\int_0^\infty k(t)D_g(t)dt,
\]
\[
\Delta(t)=\sum_{2\le n\le e^t}\Lambda(n)-(e^t-1),
\quad
\mathcal S(g)=2\int_0^\infty\Delta(t)e^{-t/2}
 (C_g'(t)-C_g(t)/2)dt.
\]
For the smooth class of L4 these are absolutely convergent. Splitting
\(d\psi(e^t)=e^t dt+d\Delta(t)\), combining the continuous part with both pole functionals, and integrating by parts gives
\[
Q(g)=\mathcal J(g)-d_AH(g)+\mathcal S(g).             \tag{JS}
\]
Indeed the pole contribution is
\(2\int_0^\infty(e^{t/2}+e^{-t/2})C_g(t)dt\);
\(2\int e^{-t/2}C_g=4H-\int e^{-t/2}D_g\);
and \(-2\int e^{-t/2}C_g\,d\Delta=\mathcal S(g)\).
The boundary at zero vanishes because \(\Delta(0)=0\); the boundary at infinity vanishes by (ENV), or by L4's correlation estimate. This rederives [V, (9)].

Parseval with the convention (FT) gives exactly the request's normalized correlation,
\[
C_{f_0}(t)=
 \frac{\int_{\mathbb R}\Xi(y)^2\cos(yt)dy}
      {\int_{\mathbb R}\Xi(y)^2dy}.
\]
It follows that
\[
\boxed{\mathcal J(f_0)+\mathcal S(f_0)=d_A.}          \tag{CAN-EQ}
\]
These are explicit convergent integrals, not evaluations at numerical zeros. □

### L1c. Actual CCM Fourier-projection recovery [COFINAL_FAMILY][PAPER]

The compact fixed-test theorem (REC) cannot simply be applied to \(f_0\). The following estimates repair that use.

Let \(m\to\infty\) through integers, \(L=\log m\), \(N=m\). Let P be the L² projection on the literal full modes
\[
L^{-1/2}e^{2\pi in(x+L/2)/L}\mathbf1_{[-L/2,L/2]},\qquad |n|\le m,
\]
followed by zero extension. Then
\[
\|Pf_0-f_0\|_X\longrightarrow0,
\quad \|Pf_0-f_0\|_2\longrightarrow0,
\quad Q(Pf_0)\longrightarrow0.                      \tag{REC-XI}
\]
No positivity of the finite matrices is asserted.

**Proof with explicit budget.** Take the cutoffs from the domain lemma and \(R=L/4\), with \(L>4\). Set \(u_R=\chi_Rf_0\). All cutoff derivative bounds \(c_j\) are independent of R. Consequently
\[
C_4:=\sum_{j=0}^4\binom4j c_{4-j}\|f_0^{(j)}\|_1
\]
is a finite uniform bound for \(\|u_R^{(4)}\|_1\), where \(c_0=1\).
Four integrations by parts in each nonzero Fourier coefficient, followed by summation of the tails and their first derivatives, give
\[
\|Pu_R-u_R\|_{\infty,J_L},\quad
\|(Pu_R-u_R)'\|_{\infty,J_L}
 \le\epsilon_c:=C_4(L/m)^2.                         \tag{RC}
\]
For any piecewise smooth error h on \(J_L\), zero extended, with both interior bounds at most \(\epsilon\),
\[
\operatorname{TV}(h)\le(L+2)\epsilon,
\quad D_h(t)\le\min(2(L+2)\epsilon^2t,4L\epsilon^2).
\]
The 2 includes both endpoint jumps. Using
\(a(t)\le4/(3t)\) for \(0<t\le1\) and
\(a(t)\le(4/3)e^{-t/2}\) for \(t\ge1\), one obtains
\[
\|h\|_X\le B_L\epsilon,
\qquad B_L=\sqrt{mL+14(L+2)}.                       \tag{RB}
\]
The weighted part costs at most \(mL\epsilon^2\). Thus the sharp endpoints have not been treated as H¹ endpoints.

For \(r_R=(1-\chi_R)f_0\), (ENV) gives
\[
\|r_R\|_1\le T_R:=\frac{A_0}{a e^{2R}}e^{-a e^{2R}}.
\]
Every coefficient of its restriction to \(J_L\) has modulus at most \(L^{-1/2}T_R\). Therefore
\[
\|Pr_R\|_\infty\le\frac{2m+1}{L}T_R,\qquad
\|(Pr_R)'\|_\infty\le\frac{2\pi m}{L}\frac{2m+1}{L}T_R.
\]
Put
\[
\epsilon_t=\frac{2m+1}{L}T_R\max(1,2\pi m/L).
\]
Finally, if \(c_1\) bounds \(|\chi_R'|\), the elementary energy estimate
\(\mathcal D(h)\le(2/3)\|h'\|_2^2+(32/3)\|h\|_2^2\)
and (ENV) imply
\[
\|r_R\|_X\le C_{\rm cut}e^{-a e^{2R}},\quad
C_{\rm cut}^2=\frac{(35/3)A_0^2+(4/3)(A_1^2+c_1^2A_0^2)}{2a}.
\]
Since \(Pf_0-f_0=(Pu_R-u_R)+Pr_R-r_R\), the full budget is
\[
\boxed{\|Pf_0-f_0\|_X
\le B_L(\epsilon_c+\epsilon_t)+C_{\rm cut}e^{-a\sqrt m}.} \tag{REC-BUDGET}
\]
Here \(e^{2R}=\sqrt m\); every term tends to zero. By (CONT),
\[
|Q(Pf_0)-Q(f_0)|\le C_X\|Pf_0-f_0\|_X
 (2\|f_0\|_X+\|Pf_0-f_0\|_X),
\]
which proves (REC-XI). □

This proof uses a uniform derivative budget for the moving cutoff, then a separate bound for its omitted tail. It does not promote fixed-test recovery to unrestricted moving-ground-state recovery. It uses the existing diagonal \(m=N\); it does not change the route's schedule.

\[
Q(f_0+\alpha v)=Q(f_0)+2\Re(\alpha B(f_0,v))+|\alpha|^2Q(v).
\]
Equations (NULL) and (RAD) give (VAR). □

The domain is now explicit. This establishes the assertion for every v in X, including every compact-window form-domain vector. It does not claim a pairing on an undefined maximal global domain.

## L3. Second variation: an exact canonical geometric representation

### L3a. Canonical-weighted differences [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

Let \(r\in\mathbb C+C_c^\infty(\mathbb R;\mathbb C)\): outside a compact set r is one constant. Set \(v=f_0r\) and
\[
E_r(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}f_0(x)f_0(x+t)|r(x+t)-r(x)|^2dx\ge0.
\]
Then the exact **ground-state transform**, meaning a change of variables using the positive radical vector rather than an assumed minimizer, is
\[
\boxed{
Q(f_0r)=\int_0^\infty b(t)E_r(t)dt
             +\sum_{n\ge2}w_nE_r(\log n),
\quad b(t)=k(t)-e^{t/2}.}                           \tag{GS}
\]
All integrals and sums in (GS) converge separately. No zeros appear in its right-hand side. It covers every original compact smooth v because \(f_0>0\), so \(r=v/f_0\) is again compact smooth.

**Proof.** Put \(w=f_0|r|^2\). Both v and w are a constant multiple of \(f_0\) plus a compact smooth function, hence belong to X. Subtract \(\Re B(f_0,w)=0\) from \(Q(v)\).
For positive real \(p=f_0(x)\), \(q=f_0(x+t)\), and complex \(u=r(x)\), \(z=r(x+t)\), the identity
\[
|qz-pu|^2-(q-p)(q|z|^2-p|u|^2)=pq|z-u|^2
\]
shows that the translation-energy difference contributes \(a(t)E_r(t)\). The scalar diagonal cancels because
\(\langle f_0,w\rangle=\|v\|_2^2\).
For the pole correlation the corresponding difference is
\[
2pq\Re(\bar u z)-pq(|u|^2+|z|^2)=-pq|z-u|^2.
\]
The pole contribution therefore contributes
\(-(e^{t/2}+e^{-t/2})E_r(t)\), while the negative prime pairing contributes \(+w_nE_r(\log n)\). Combining these terms yields
\(a-e^{-t/2}-e^{t/2}=k-e^{t/2}\), proving (GS).
Near zero, \(E_r(t)=O(t^2)\), since r has a bounded derivative. Far away, a difference can be nonzero only when at least one argument is in the fixed compact support of the nonconstant part of r. The other canonical factor then has the super-exponential decay (ENV). These bounds justify every subtraction and integration. □

**Proposed Lean head:** `xidev_canonical_weighted_difference_identity (r : ℝ → ℂ)`, with hypotheses `ContDiff ℝ ⊤ r` and `∃ c, HasCompactSupport (r - fun _ => c)`, and conclusion (GS). This head must retain the signed b, not replace it by its positive part.

### L3b. Exact obstruction to a manifestly positive jump representation [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

The continuous density in (GS) is not nonnegative. Writing \(u=e^t\),
\[
b(t)=\sqrt u\left(\frac1{u^3-u}-1\right).
\]
On \(I=[\log(7/5),\log(8/5)]\) there is no prime-power atom, and
\(u^3-u\ge168/125\). Consequently the full signed measure
\[
d\nu(t)=b(t)dt+\sum_{n\ge2}w_n\delta_{\log n}
\]
satisfies the strict upper bound
\[
\boxed{\nu(I)\le-\frac{43}{168}\log(8/7)<0.}        \tag{NEG-MEASURE}
\]
This is a counterexample to positivity of that representing measure. It is not a counterexample to positivity of the Weil form: the functions \(E_r\) are constrained coupled differences, not arbitrary nonnegative test functions of t.

The repaired, source-specific inequality is exactly
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\ge2}w_nE_r(\log n)+\int_0^\infty b_+(t)E_r(t)dt
\ \ge\ \int_0^\infty b_-(t)E_r(t)dt
\quad\text{for all }r\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R;\mathbb C),}
\tag{DOM}
\]
where \(b_+=\max(b,0)\), \(b_-=\max(-b,0)\). All terms are finite and independently defined. This proof has not established (DOM). Discarding its negative integral or admitting arbitrary nonnegative functions in place of \(E_r\) would change the theorem.

**Proposed Lean head:** `xidev_canonical_jump_measure_negative_interval`, with the exact rational interval and upper bound (NEG-MEASURE).

### L3c. Orthogonal deviations and the unchanged Hessian [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

Use the explicitly defined X inner product, and put
\[
\Pi g=g-\frac{\langle f_0,g\rangle_X}{\|f_0\|_X^2}f_0.
\]
Then \(\Pi g\perp_X f_0\) and
\[
Q(\Pi g)=Q(g).                                     \tag{ORTH}
\]
This follows immediately by expansion and (RAD). For compact smooth g, \(\Pi g=f_0r\) with r equal to a constant outside a compact set, so (GS) applies and its energy is unchanged by that constant.

For smooth deviations in \(\mathscr E_a\) of L4 (in particular the projected compact tests just considered), the request's coordinates give
\[
Q(v)=\mathcal J(v)-d_A\|v\|_2^2+\mathcal S(v),
\qquad
\mathcal S(v)=2\int_0^\infty e^{t/2}C_v(t)dt
                   -2\sum_{n\ge2}w_nC_v(\log n).   \tag{S-GEOM}
\]
Equivalently, the requested archimedean-like splitting is
\[
\mathcal S(v)=\int_0^\infty e^{-t/2}D_v(t)dt
 +Q_{\rm pole}(v)-4\|v\|_2^2
 -2\sum_{n\ge2}w_nC_v(\log n).
\]
The first displayed energy and \(\mathcal J\) are nonnegative; the complete remaining signed combination is not bounded below by this fact. On that admissible smooth class, formula (EF) identifies the same Hessian as
\(\sum_z\overline{\mathcal Fv(\bar z)}\mathcal Fv(z)\).
For a nonreal conjugate pair its contribution is a real cross product, not a squared modulus. The modulus-square expression would follow from RH; it is not used as a premise.

For a structural plant take
\[
Q_*=\operatorname{diag}(0,-1,1),\quad
J_*=\operatorname{diag}(1/2,0,2),\quad
S_*=\operatorname{diag}(1/2,0,0),\quad d_*=1.
\]
Then \(Q_*=J_*-d_*I+S_*\), both \(J_*\) and \(S_*\) are positive semidefinite, and \(e_1\) is a radical vector. Nevertheless
\[
\boxed{Q_*(e_2)=-1<0,\qquad e_2\perp e_1.}         \tag{PLANT}
\]
Thus even a stronger abstract sign decomposition and a stationary canonical vector do not certify a minimum. The actual form is not refuted by this auxiliary matrix.

**Proposed Lean heads:** `xidev_radical_projection_preserves_weilQ` with (ORTH), and `xidev_radical_not_minimum_plant` with (PLANT). No assumption that the radical equals the one-dimensional canonical line is made or needed.

## L4. A dense smooth class and a signed finite-prime reduction

### L4a. The correct class [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

Fix \(0<a\le\pi/2\). Define \(\mathscr E_a\) to consist of all complex C∞ functions g such that for every derivative order j there is a finite constant \(A_j(g)\) with
\[
|g^{(j)}(x)|\le A_j(g)e^{-a e^{2|x|}}\quad(x\in\mathbb R).
\tag{CLASS}
\]
This is a vector space contained in X, contains every compact smooth test and \(f_0\), and is dense in X. Its projection \(\Pi\mathscr E_a\) is dense in \(X\cap f_0^{\perp_X}\) and remains in \(\mathscr E_a\).

**Proof.** The estimates imply weighted integrability and finite translation energy by the derivative estimate used in L1c. Closure under sums and scalars follows by adding/scaling the constants. Compact support makes each derivative bound finite on its support; (ENV) covers \(f_0\). The domain lemma proves density, and continuous projection proves projected density. □

The constants are allowed to depend on g. A ball with fixed constants is not asserted dense, and a common prime cutoff over the entire class is not asserted.

**Correction to [R, L4].** The example "\(\Xi^2\) of order one and finite type" has the wrong type condition. For real \(s\to+\infty\), the positive Dirichlet series gives \(\zeta(s)\ge1\), and the Gamma integral gives
\(\Gamma(s/2)\ge e^{-s/2-1}(s/2)^{s/2-1}\).
The functional equation then shows
\[
\log|\Xi(i(s-1/2))^2|\ge s\log s-O(s).
\]
Thus no bound of the form \(Ce^{T|z|}\) holds globally for \(\Xi(z)^2\). The super-exponential correlation estimate below comes from (CLASS), not from that false finite-type description. No zero-free region is used in this argument.

**Proposed Lean head:** `xidev_superexp_class_dense`, with fixed a, function-dependent derivative constants, and the density/projection conclusions above.

### L4b. Explicit super-exponential correlation bound [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

For \(g\in\mathscr E_a\), let \(A_0,A_1\) be witnesses in (CLASS), and set
\[
M_g=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2\sqrt a}
       (A_0A_1+A_0^2/2).
\]
For all \(t\ge0\),
\[
\boxed{|C_g'(t)-C_g(t)/2|
 \le M_g e^{-t/2}e^{-2ae^t}.}                       \tag{CORR}
\]

**Proof.** Differentiation under the correlation integral is justified by (CLASS). Its integrands are bounded using
\[
e^{2|x|}+e^{2|x+t|}
 \ge e^{-2x}+e^{2(x+t)}
 =2e^t\cosh(2x+t).
\]
Since \(\cosh(2y)\ge1+2y^2\),
\[
\int e^{-a(e^{2|x|}+e^{2|x+t|})}dx
 \le \frac{\sqrt\pi}{2\sqrt a}\,e^{-t/2}e^{-2ae^t}.
\]
Multiply this bound by \(A_0^2\) for \(|C_g|\) and by \(A_0A_1\) for \(|C_g'|\), then add with the factor 1/2. □

**Proposed Lean head:** `xidev_correlation_superexp_bound`, with precisely the two envelope hypotheses and conclusion (CORR).

### L4c. Finite primes, correct endpoint, and explicit tail [COFINAL_FAMILY][PAPER]

For an integer \(P\ge2\), put \(T=\log P\) and
\[
\begin{split}
S_P(g)={}&2\int_0^T e^{t/2}C_g(t)dt
 -2\sum_{2\le n\le P}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}C_g(\log n)\\
&+2\Delta(T)P^{-1/2}C_g(T).
\end{split}                                                    \tag{SP}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{|\mathcal S(g)-S_P(g)|\le
E_S(g,P):=\frac{M_g}{a}\frac{1+\log P}{P}e^{-2aP}.} \tag{TAIL-S}
\]
The entire signed finite prime sum is retained. In particular the term
\(2\Delta(T)P^{-1/2}C_g(T)\) must not be dropped when truncating the discrepancy integral.

**Proof.** On \([0,T]\) apply Stieltjes integration by parts to
\(2\int\Delta(t)d(e^{-t/2}C_g(t))\), using the right-continuous \(\psi\). Its jump measure has atoms \(\Lambda(n)\) at \(\log n\). This gives exactly (SP), including an atom at P when P is a prime power. The term at zero is zero.
The elementary estimate
\[
|\Delta(t)|\le e^t(1+t)
\]
follows from \(0\le\psi(x)\le x\log x\). By (CORR),
\[
|\mathcal S-S_P|
\le2M_g\int_T^\infty(1+t)e^{-2ae^t}dt
=2M_g\int_P^\infty\frac{1+\log x}{x}e^{-2ax}dx.
\]
The factor \((1+\log x)/x\) decreases for \(x\ge1\). Bounding it by its value at P and integrating the exponential proves (TAIL-S). □

For the canonical unit test this holds in particular with \(a=\pi/2\) and the explicit \(A_0,A_1\) from (ENV); its bound at P=59 contains \(e^{-59\pi}\). This is a full infinite-tail bound, not a confirmation of an observed signed decimal from a finite interval.

To make all integrals finite as well, put
\(J_P(g)=\int_0^{\log P} k(t)D_g(t)dt\). Since \(0\le D_g(t)\le4H(g)\),
\[
0\le\mathcal J(g)-J_P(g)\le
E_J(g,P):=\frac{8H(g)}{5(1-P^{-2})}P^{-5/2}.         \tag{TAIL-J}
\]
This follows by bounding the denominator of k on \([\log P,\infty)\) and integrating \(e^{-5t/2}\).

Define the finite arithmetic-and-integral expression
\[
\mathcal A_P(g)=J_P(g)+S_P(g)-d_AH(g).
\]
The required two-sided certificate is
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_P(g)-E_S(g,P)\le Q(g)
\le\mathcal A_P(g)+E_S(g,P)+E_J(g,P).}              \tag{CERT}
\]
Only the tails have been estimated in absolute value; the potentially decisive cancellation inside \(\mathcal A_P\) has not been replaced by an absolute sum.

An explicit cutoff for any \(\varepsilon>0\) is any integer at least
\[
P(g,\varepsilon)=\left\lceil\max\left\{
2,\left(\frac{64H(g)}{15\varepsilon}\right)^{2/5},
\frac1{2a}\log\left(1+\frac{2M_g}{a\varepsilon}\right)
\right\}\right\rceil.                            \tag{PCUT}
\]
Indeed \((1+\log P)/P\le1\) and \((1-P^{-2})^{-1}\le4/3\) give
\(E_S\le\varepsilon/2\) and \(E_J\le\varepsilon/2\).

**Proposed Lean heads:** `xidev_signed_prime_cutoff_identity` for (SP), `xidev_signed_prime_cutoff_error` for (TAIL-S), and `xidev_finite_margin_enclosure` for (CERT) and (PCUT). Endpoint conventions, the full prime-power sum, and both different error signs are part of their statements.
