## 0. Decision and sources

**The right consumer is the full Weil form, not necessarily the nonnegative margin.** The addendum is controlling about the instrument: the two negative full-margin decimals in the request are withdrawn as sign evidence. Neither their sign nor extrapolation to zero is used below. Q1(a) remains a paper question; this verdict does not obtain its requested eventual sign or a coefficient for the correction square. There is nevertheless a stronger result for the actual consumer of this cell. Section 3 proves directly, from the exact geometric Weil formula and elementary inequalities,
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal Q(v)\ge\frac1{100}\|v\|_2^2}
 \tag{P0}
\]
for every smooth complex function supported in the union of the two prescribed lobe intervals whose **total** two pole moments vanish. The two profiles are independent. Their separate pole moments need not vanish. No Sonin inverse, truncation, gauge certificate, or sign assumption on the full margin is used in this new proof. It is a PAPER derivation supplied for independent checking, not a Lean result. The earlier minus-class inequality \(Q\ge n_2+\mathcal F\) is stronger in a different direction and is retained, not replaced. [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

The principal order of Q and of its reservoir is logarithmic. It is not the order-minus-one dyadic principal kernel of the scalar floor. Positivity of their high-frequency principal matrices by itself does not prove the whole-class sign. The direct proof supplies the missing low-frequency and moment control as well. No all-support or RH claim follows from this one fixed union of intervals.

## 1. Convention and target lock

Put
\[
 a=\log2,\quad r=2^{-1/2},\quad w=ar,\quad
 \delta=\frac{\log3-\log2}{8},\quad I=(-\delta,\delta).
\]
Let \(U_sh(x)=h(x-s)\), \(\widehat h(\xi)=\int h(x)e^{-i\xi x}dx\), and use inner products antilinear in the first argument. On smooth compact tests define
\[
 C_f(t)=\Re\int\overline{f(x)}f(x+t)dx,\qquad
 A(t)=\frac{e^{-t/2}}{1-e^{-2t}}\quad(t>0),
\]
\[
 \mathcal D(f)=\int_0^\infty A(t)\|U_tf-f\|_2^2dt,
 \qquad c_A=\gamma_E+\log(8\pi)+\frac\pi2.
 \tag{P1}
\]
In the source conventions the full Weil formula is
\[
 \mathcal Q(f)=\mathcal D(f)-c_A\|f\|^2
 -2\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}C_f(\log n)
 +2\Re\{M_+(f)\overline{M_-(f)}\},
 \quad M_\pm(f)=\int f(x)e^{\pm x/2}dx.
 \tag{P2}
\]
This is the geometric source formula of ST/R. It also follows from the digamma integral and the finite-prime Fourier correlations; no zero-side positivity is assumed. The sign of the last term matters outside the pole-null space.

Write
\[
 v=U_{a/2}h_1+U_{-a/2}h_2,\qquad h_1,h_2\in C_c^\infty(I;\mathbb C),
 \quad H=\|h_1\|^2+\|h_2\|^2=\|v\|^2.
 \tag{P3}
\]
The full profile space needed for this cell is
\[
 \mathscr H_{\rm tot}=\{(h_1,h_2):
 e^{a/4}M_+(h_1)+e^{-a/4}M_+(h_2)=0,
 \ e^{-a/4}M_-(h_1)+e^{a/4}M_-(h_2)=0\}.
 \tag{P4}
\]
It has two global constraints. \(H_{00}\oplus H_{00}\) is a smaller, four-constraint subspace. Both are legitimate, but proving a theorem only on the latter must not be described as proving all total-pole-null profiles. The new bound (P0) covers (P4).

Since the support diameter is \(a+2\delta<\log3\), the only nonzero prime-power correlation in (P2) can be n=2. The pole term vanishes on (P4). Therefore
\[
 \mathcal Q(v)=L_2(v)
 =\mathcal D(v)-c_AH-2w\Re\langle h_1,h_2\rangle.
 \tag{P5}
\]
The source tested identities additionally give, on their smooth domain,
\[
 L_2(v)=n_2(v)+\mathfrak m(v),\quad
 n_2(v)=\|T_v\mathsf S_2\|_{HS}^2,
\]
\[
 \mathfrak m(v)=-\int d_2(\xi)|\widehat v(\xi)|^2d\xi
 =Q_{\rm sc}[v]+\|T_vD_2\|_{HS}^2,
 \quad Q_{\rm sc}[v]=-\int\ell_2(\xi)|\widehat v(\xi)|^2d\xi.
 \tag{P6}
\]
These are equalities, not independent lower bounds. A nonnegative reservoir alone never proves Q nonnegative. Conversely a negative margin alone would not refute Q. [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

## 2. Q1(a,b): principal order, exact matrix, and the still-open margin coefficient

### 2.1 The correction square and what the addendum withdraws

For the source compression \(A_2\) of R, put \(Z=(I-A_2^2)^{-1}\), and let \(u_2(\xi),t_2(\xi),\gamma_2(\xi)\) be exactly R (5)--(6). If \(\gamma_2=e^{i\theta}\), write
\(e^{-i\theta/2}u_2=X+iY\), with X,Y real. Direct expansion gives
\[
 s_2(\xi):=\ell_2(\xi)-d_2(\xi)
 =2\langle X,(I+A_2)^{-1}X\rangle
  +2\langle Y,(I-A_2)^{-1}Y\rangle\ge0.
 \tag{P7}
\]
A choice of the square root of \(\gamma_2\) does not change this value. This is the positive source density of the correction square, not of the margin itself.

For fixed nonzero smooth \(\eta\in C_c^\infty(I)\), define
\[
 h_T=(\partial^2-1/4)(e^{iTx}\eta),\quad
 v_{+,T}=\frac{U_{a/2}h_T+U_{-a/2}h_T}{\sqrt2\|h_T\|}.
\]
Both pole moments vanish. GAUGE (G34), at its PAPER status, gives
\[
 Q_{\rm sc}[v_{+,T}]=-\frac{1+C_a}{c}p(T)+o(T^{-1}),
 \quad C_a=\cosh(a/2),\ c=C_a-1,
 \tag{P8}
\]
where p is SCH's positive dyadic multiplier and p(T) is bounded above and below by positive constants times 1/T.

The exact remaining ratio is
\[
 \mathcal C_T=\frac1{p(T)\|h_T\|^2}
  \int(1+\cos a\xi)|\widehat h_T(\xi)|^2s_2(\xi)d\xi\ge0,
\]
\[
 \boxed{\frac{\mathfrak m(v_{+,T})}{p(T)}
       =\mathcal C_T-\frac{1+C_a}{c}+o(1).}
 \tag{P9}
\]
No coefficient or limit for \(\mathcal C_T\) is proved here. R's source decay \(d_2=O(|\xi|^{-1/2})\) gives only
\[
 |\mathfrak m(v_{+,T})|=O_\eta(T^{-1/2}),\qquad
 \|T_{v_{+,T}}D_2\|_{HS}^2=O_\eta(T^{-1/2}).
 \tag{P10}
\]
To verify this passage, write \(T^{-2}e^{-iTx}h_T=-\eta+2i\eta'/T+(\eta''-\eta/4)/T^2\); this is a uniformly Schwartz family with a nonzero limiting norm. Split its Fourier integral into |xi-T|<=T/2 and the rapidly decaying complement, and use boundedness of d2 at low frequency. Apply (P6),(P8) for the square. This does not improve the order to 1/T.

Thus the requested eventual-negative theorem is **not supplied**. A strict upper bound \(\limsup\mathcal C_T<(1+C_a)/c\) would prove it; a strict reverse lower bound would prove eventual positivity. Equality at that threshold requires another term, and log-periodic subsequences may matter. Neither the J8 table nor positivity of (P7) resolves (P9). This is the explicit discriminator for the zero-consistent margin. [ABSTRACT][CONDITIONAL for the unresolved sign; PAPER for (P7)--(P10)]

### 2.2 Full Weil matrix in log coordinates

Set
\[
 q_\infty(\xi)=\Re\psi(1/4+i\xi/2)-\log\pi,
 \quad q_2=q_\infty-2a\sum_{j\ge1}r^j\cos(ja\xi).
\]
Then \(L_2(v)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int q_2|\widehat v|^2\). The inverse Fourier distribution \(K_\infty=\check q_\infty\) equals \(-A(|t|)\) away from t=0. Its contact/finite-part term is exactly the diagonal form in (P1), not an omitted constant.

For \(t=x-y\in I-I\), the exact polarized profile kernel of Q is
\[
 \boxed{\mathbf K_Q(t)=K_\infty(t)I_2
 -w\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\delta_0(t)
 -\begin{pmatrix}0&A(a+t)\\A(a-t)&0\end{pmatrix}.}
 \tag{P11}
\]
Indeed its ij entry is the full source kernel at t+x_i-x_j, with x1=a/2, x2=-a/2. The sole shifted prime delta is the one at a; higher prime powers and all primes >=3 are outside the difference support. The two smooth off-diagonal functions need not agree. The matrix satisfies \(\mathbf K_Q(-t)=\mathbf K_Q(t)^*\), as required.

Choose a smooth compact cutoff equal to one on I-I and supported strictly within (-a,a) for the last matrix in (P11). Its Fourier transform decays faster than every inverse power. The local matrix multiplier is consequently
\[
 \mathbf q_Q(\xi)=q_\infty(\xi)I_2-wJ+\mathbf e(\xi),
 \quad J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
 \quad \mathbf e(\xi)=O_N(|\xi|^{-N})\quad\hbox{for all }N.
 \tag{P12}
\]
The digamma asymptotic gives
\[
 \boxed{\mathbf q_Q(\xi)
 =\log(|\xi|/(2\pi))I_2-wJ+O(|\xi|^{-2}).}
 \tag{P13}
\]
Its principal symmetric and antisymmetric channels have eigenvalues respectively \(\log(|\xi|/(2\pi))-w\) and \(\log(|\xi|/(2\pi))+w\). Both are positive at sufficiently high frequency. The plus channel is the smaller one there. These are principal channels; the smooth odd cross kernel can mix them at finite frequency. A constant two-channel rotation does not diagonalize every exact mixed term for arbitrary profiles. [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

### 2.3 The reservoir's principal matrix is of the same logarithmic order

In the fixed source normalization,
\[
 2\pi k_2=q_2+2\pi d_2.
\]
Therefore the exact reservoir matrix kernel is
\[
 (\mathbf K_n)_{ij}(t)=(\mathbf K_Q)_{ij}(t)
       +2\pi\check d_2(t+x_i-x_j),
 \quad (\mathbf K_{\mathfrak m})_{ij}=-2\pi\check d_2(t+x_i-x_j).
 \tag{P14}
\]
These formulas are interpreted after testing, on the previously proved source trace domain. The d2 compression on a bounded union of intervals is compact: approximate the bounded multiplier d2 by its frequency cutoff; the cutoff compression is Hilbert--Schmidt, and the remaining operator norm tends to zero because d2 tends to zero. Its translated block compressions have the same property.

Thus reservoir and full Weil form have the same logarithmic principal matrix (P13). For the normalized fixed-envelope modulations,
\[
 \mathcal Q(v_{\pm,T})=\log(T/(2\pi))\mp w+o(1),
 \qquad n_2(v_{\pm,T})=\log(T/(2\pi))\mp w+o(1).
 \tag{P15}
\]
To obtain the exact constant prime term, use the autocorrelation support: C_v(a)=+1/2 or -1/2. The shifted archimedean cross integral is the Fourier transform of a smooth compact function at T, with uniformly bounded envelope derivatives, and tends to zero. The diagonal digamma integral gives log(T/(2pi))+o(1). Equation (P10), also for the minus profile, gives the second assertion.

It is a category error to assign Q the same decaying dyadic principal multiplier p as the scalar floor. In particular Q minus p times any fixed matrix cannot have a W1,1 compact-extension remainder: on these modulations that alleged representation would be O(1/T), whereas (P15) grows logarithmically. The reservoir carries the logarithmic part; the unknown margin is a smaller difference. Neither a reservoir/margin 1/T cancellation coefficient nor an all-frequency matrix sign follows from (P15). [ABSTRACT][PAPER; THEOREM_SHAPE]

## 3. Q1(d): a direct whole-two-profile theorem with a rational lower envelope

### 3.1 Statement

**Theorem P1.** Let h1,h2 be complex smooth functions supported in I, let v be (P3), and assume only (P4). Then
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal Q(v)\ge\frac1{100}(\|h_1\|^2+\|h_2\|^2).}
 \tag{P16}
\]
This includes every pair of individually pole-null profiles, every mixed pairing via the Hermitian quadratic statement, and in particular both same-envelope phase channels. The proof is independent of the GAUGE certificate and of a sign for \(\mathfrak m\). It uses the exact formula (P2), not a finite matrix surrogate. [ABSTRACT][PAPER; new derivation, independent review pending]

### 3.2 Total pole moments control the two local means

Set \(d=13/125\). Elementary logarithm bounds give \(2\delta<d\) and \(2d<a\). The profiles are consequently supported inside [-d/2,d/2]. Put \(m_i=\int h_i\), \(m=(m_1,m_2)^t\), and
\[
 V=\begin{pmatrix}e^{a/4}&e^{-a/4}\\e^{-a/4}&e^{a/4}\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The moment equations say Vm=-e, where each component of e is the corresponding weighted integral of \(h_i(e^{\pm x/2}-1)\). With
\(\eta_d=e^{d/4}-1\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \|e\|_{\mathbb C^2}\le (e^{a/4}+e^{-a/4})\eta_d\sqrt{dH}.
\]
The smaller singular value of V is \(e^{a/4}-e^{-a/4}\). Therefore
\[
 \|m\|^2\le d\coth^2(a/4)\eta_d^2H<\frac d{25}H.
 \tag{P17}
\]
For explicit rational verification, \(\coth(a/4)=3+2\sqrt2<6\) and
\(\eta_d\le(d/4)/(1-d/4)=13/487\), whence \(6\eta_d<1/5\). The bound is for the two *global* moment equations, not four local equations.

**Falsifier.** Without (P4), take h1 tending smoothly in L2 to 1_I and h2=0. Then \(|m_1|^2/\|h_1\|^2\to2\delta>d/25\), contradicting (P17). The discarded moment constraints are genuinely used; there is no unconditional small-mean assertion.

### 3.3 Exact diagonal and cross energy bookkeeping

For each supported profile,
\[
 \int_0^d\|U_th_i-h_i\|^2dt=2d\|h_i\|^2-|m_i|^2.
 \tag{P18}
\]
This follows by integrating its autocorrelation; \(\int_{-d}^d C_{h_i}(t)dt=|m_i|^2\). For t>=d the autocorrelation is zero. A(t) decreases strictly, so
\[
 \sum_i\mathcal D(h_i)\ge
 \left(2dA(d)+2\int_d^\infty A(t)dt\right)H-A(d)\|m\|^2.
 \tag{P19}
\]
For v the sole additional archimedean cross correlation lies at t in [a-d,a+d]. Its absolute value is at most \(\|h_1\|\|h_2\|\). Writing
\[
 J_d=\int_{a-d}^{a+d}A(t)dt,
\]
its total negative cost is at most \(2J_d\|h_1\|\|h_2\|\le J_dH\). The prime cost is at most wH. Thus (P5) gives
\[
 \mathcal Q(v)\ge
 \left[2dA(d)+2\int_d^\infty A-c_A-J_d-w-\frac d{25}A(d)\right]H.
 \tag{P20}
\]
No high-mode asymptotic or continuum-to-finite passage occurs in this inequality.

### 3.4 Rational arithmetic proves the bracket is positive

Here are explicit bounds with deliberately ample rounding; they are proved analytically, not read from a numerical scan.

First,
\[
 2dA(d)\ge1,\qquad dA(d)<3/5.
 \tag{P21}
\]
Indeed \(2dA(d)=e^{d/2}/(\sinh d/d)\). For 0<d<=1,
\(\sinh d/d\le(1-d^2/6)^{-1}\), by \((2n+1)!\ge6^n\), and
\((1+d/2)(1-d^2/6)\ge1\). The upper bound follows from
\(1-e^{-2d}\ge2d(1-d)\), so \(dA(d)\le1/[2(1-d)]<3/5\).

With y=exp(-d/2), elementary integration gives
\[
 2\int_d^\infty A(t)dt
 =\log\frac{1+y}{1-y}+2\arctan y
 \ge\log(4/d)+\frac\pi2-\frac d2.
 \tag{P22}
\]
Use \(\tanh(d/4)\le d/4\) for the first term. The derivative of \(2\arctan e^{-d/2}\) is \(-1/[2\cosh(d/2)]\ge-1/2\) for the second. Consequently
\[
 2\int_d^\infty A-c_A\ge-\gamma_E-\log(2\pi d)-d/2.
\]
The usable rational estimates are
\[
 \gamma_E<29/50,\quad 2\pi d<2/3,\quad
 \log(3/2)>152/375,\quad d/2=13/250.
 \tag{P23}

### 6.2 A precise local extraction that avoids a false uniform kappa

Here is a source-series lemma; it supplies convergence, not positivity. Let U be any of the seven compact log-lag neighborhoods needed by the three profiles, and let U+ be a fixed larger interval with a positive distance between its endpoints and U. In each difference term list its exact root
\(\tau=\log(\beta_\nu/\beta_\mu)\), or its negative in the opposite branch.
For every root in U+, extract the **linearized resonant kernel**
\[
 e^{\tau/2}L(\beta_\nu(t-\tau)),\qquad
 L(z)=\operatorname{Si}(z)/z,
 \tag{P32}
\]
with that term's original coefficient; use the reflected version for the opposite branch. Keep the sum arguments and roots outside U+ in the remainder. Group the resulting terms by shells
\(n\le\log\max(\beta_\nu,\beta_\mu)<n+1\).

**Lemma P2 (all-resonance local extraction).** On U, the extracted principal series converges in L1. The remainder series converges in W1,1. For constants depending only on U,U+ and the fixed two-prime coefficients, the tails beyond shell M satisfy bounds of the forms
\[
 \|\mathcal P_{>M}\|_{L^1(U)}
       \le C_U\sum_{n\ge M}(n+2)^5e^{-n},
\]
\[
 \|\mathcal R_{>M}\|_{W^{1,1}(U)}
       \le C'_U\sum_{n\ge M}(n+2)^4e^{-n/2}.
 \tag{P33}
\]
This is an analytic statement, not a certified numerical value for the constants.

**Proof.** There are at most C(n+2)^2 frequencies below e^{n+1}, hence at most C'(n+2)^4 ordered pairs per shell; a tighter count is unnecessary. A root in the fixed U+ makes the two frequencies comparable by fixed positive constants. The elementary estimate from SCH (3) gives
\(\int_U|L(\beta(t-\tau))|dt\le C_U(1+\log\beta)/\beta\), uniformly in tau in U+. This proves the first bound.
Near its root the original first difference term is
\(e^{\tau/2}e^{z/2}L(\beta_\nu(e^z-1))\), z=t-tau, using L's evenness. Subtract (P32): the difference is exactly \(e^{\tau/2}q_{\beta_\nu}(z)\). SCH (8) bounds its W1,1 norm by C beta^{-1/2} for |z|<=a/4. Outside that small interval but in a fixed bounded z-range, |e^z-1| is bounded away from zero and the large-argument L,L' estimates give C_U/beta. Thus the same C_U beta^{-1/2} estimate holds over the full required bounded range. Reflect for the second branch.
For roots outside U+, the positive log separation from U implies argument size at least a fixed multiple of max(beta_nu,beta_mu). Each value and first derivative costs C_U/max(beta_nu,beta_mu); the sum-argument terms satisfy the same bound. Multiply by the polynomial shell count and sum. This proves (P33), retaining every negative coefficient, low-index exception, translate and normalization. QED. [ABSTRACT][PAPER]
