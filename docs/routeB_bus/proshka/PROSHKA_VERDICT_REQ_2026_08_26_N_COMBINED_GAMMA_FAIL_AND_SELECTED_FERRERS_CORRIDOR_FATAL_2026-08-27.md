## 3. Why that correction does not rescue the compact rate

Let

\[
M=U-P,
\qquad
U=W02-WR,
\qquad
r=(M-aI)q,
\]

and let `D` be the mode diagonal. Every component has its own displacement law.
The exact full identity is

\[
\Gamma=D r=(M-aI)Dq+[D,M]q.
\]

Move `D` through the prime component:

\[
-PDq=-DPq+[D,P]q.
\]

The prime commutator term cancels the prime part already present in `[D,M]q`, so

\[
\Gamma=(U-aI)Dq-DPq+[D,U]q.
\]

But the residual equation gives

\[
Pq=Uq-aq-r.
\]

Substitution yields

\[
\Gamma
=UDq-DUq+[D,U]q+Dr
=Dr.
\]

Thus the componentwise displacement identities return the defining identity.
They do not generate a contraction, a smaller functional, or a source rate.
The missed source fact repairs the anatomy but not the outcome.

This also explains why displacement rank two cannot be confused with low matrix
rank or a small Feshbach coupling. A Loewner matrix may have rank-two
**displacement** and still have full spectral rank and large operator norm.
