## 1. Q1(a): exact pole gauge and the complete local gauge freedom

### 1.1 Convention lock

Set
\[
 a=\log2,\quad \delta=(\log3-\log2)/8,\quad I=(-\delta,\delta),
 \quad d_0=a/4,\quad c=\cosh(a/2)-1,\quad c_*=2\pi ca.
\]
Let
\[
 M_\pm(h)=\int_I h(x)e^{\pm x/2}\,dx,\qquad
 H_{00}=\ker M_+\cap\ker M_-\subset L^2(I;\mathbb C).
\]
These are bounded linear functionals on this finite interval, so H00 is closed. Use the nonunitary Fourier transform `hat h(xi)=int h(x)exp(-i xi x)dx`. Write H=||h||_2^2. The unnormalized scalar form is
\[
 Q_F[h]=H\mathcal F(h)=\langle h,\mathcal T h\rangle
       =-\int(1-\cos a\xi)\ell_2(\xi)|\widehat h(\xi)|^2d\xi.
 \tag{G1}
\]
The relevant spatial kernel is SCH's `K_T=cS+R`. No extra factor 2pi is inserted or removed. In this document `F_g` denotes a Fourier transform of a remainder, not the normalized floor `mathcal F`.

### 1.2 The observer's gauge is exact, including mixed forms

For any real alpha,
\[
 \iint 2\alpha\cosh((x-y)/2)\overline{h(x)}h(y)dxdy
 =2\alpha\Re\{\overline{M_+(h)}M_-(h)\}.
 \tag{G2}
\]
The right side is zero on H00. Polarization proves the identical assertion for every mixed pairing of two H00 tests. Thus the whole compressed operator, not just individual diagonals, is unchanged. With
\[
 R_g(t)=R(t)+2\alpha\cosh(t/2),\qquad \alpha=519/1000,
 \tag{G3}
\]
and the same even quintic chi, equal to one on [-2delta,2delta] and zero outside [-d0,d0], the gauged extension has precisely the original form on H00. Gauge **before** cutting off. It would be wrong to regard the Fourier transform of the cutoff gauge as pointwise zero. Only its compressed pairings vanish. [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

The credited new step here belongs to the observer. The choice of alpha after a diagnostic search and before a frozen rigorous verification is legitimate certificate discovery. It is not a blind forecast and does not prove the reported whole interval of acceptable alpha values.

### 1.3 There are four local difference gauges, not two

**Theorem G1.** Let k be a locally integrable difference kernel on I-I, with the associated sesquilinear form defined on compact smooth tests. Its compression to H00 vanishes identically if and only if, on I-I,
\[
 k(t)=(A+Bt)e^{t/2}+(C+Dt)e^{-t/2}.
 \tag{G4}
\]
The same statement holds for distribution kernels with distributional pairings. Only the restriction to I-I is determined; values outside that interval are unconstrained.

**Proof.** Put L=partial_x^2-1/4. Every Lphi with phi compact smooth in I has both moments zero, by two integrations by parts. Vanishing of the compressed form therefore implies
\[
 L_xL_y k(x-y)=0\quad\hbox{on }I\times I.
\]
Tensor test functions determine distributions, so `(partial_t^2-1/4)^2 k=0` on I-I. One can check this locally at any difference t0 by choosing two small intervals with that difference and testing in the independent difference and center variables. The solutions of this constant-coefficient ordinary differential equation are exactly (G4). This follows by factoring the operator into `(partial-1/2)^2(partial+1/2)^2`, or successively solving the first-order distributional equations.

Conversely, the e^{±t/2} terms factor into two moments. For t e^{t/2}, expand `(x-y)e^{x/2}e^{-y/2}`. Each resulting term contains at least one ordinary pole moment of one of the two tests. It is zero on H00 x H00. The other repeated-root term is identical. This proves sufficiency without a density assumption. QED. [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

For real even difference kernels the freedom is
\[
 k(t)=A_0\cosh(t/2)+B_0\,t\sinh(t/2),\qquad A_0,B_0\in\mathbb R.
 \tag{G5}
\]
For general Hermitian difference kernels there are also the imaginary odd directions `i sinh(t/2)` and `i t cosh(t/2)`. Thus the report's claim that only alpha is available is false. The explicit falsifier is `t sinh(t/2)`, which is nonzero and invisible but is not a multiple of cosh(t/2).

For a bounded Hermitian kernel operator K, set Pe=projection onto span{e^{x/2},e^{-x/2}} and P00=I-Pe. The general, not necessarily difference-type, invisibility condition is exactly
\[
 P_{00}KP_{00}=0,
 \quad K=P_eKP_e+P_eKP_{00}+P_{00}KP_e.
 \tag{G6}
\]
These are finite-rank operators of rank at most four, allowing arbitrary two cross-profile vectors. They can improve a non-translation-invariant extension or a block estimate. They cannot improve the actual compressed margin, which is unchanged. They no longer reduce to one scalar Fourier multiplier. This distinguishes useful certificate freedom from an alteration of the inequality itself.

For T>=2pi, J=min{j:beta_j>=T} and theta=log(beta_J/T),

Since the minimum of `e^{-theta}(theta+a)` on [0,a] is a, the valid bounds are

 p(\xi)\ge ca=c_*/(2\pi)\ (0<|\xi|\le2\pi),\qquad

 \tag{G10}
\]
In particular `p >= c_*/(2pi+|xi|)` everywhere except the irrelevant point zero. At zero p has a logarithmic singularity, not a finite multiplier value. All integral assertions are almost-everywhere assertions. [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

Put `f_g=chi R_g` and `F_g=hat f_g`. If f_g is real, even and in W^{1,1}_c, then F_g is real and

 |F_g(\xi)|\le A=\|f_g\|_1,\qquad

Integration by parts has no boundary term: the absolutely continuous compact extension vanishes at both outer endpoints. This includes both transition junctions and the origin. The frequency regions meet at 2pi with no gap.

The requested thresholds A<c_*/(4pi), B<c_* are sufficient. They are not necessary. With the same split the sharper elementary thresholds are A<ca, B<c_*. Changing the split point is another allowed optimization; it is not needed here.
