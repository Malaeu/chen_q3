## 1. Q1 -- certificate audit

### 1.1 Test, homogeneity, and admissibility

Fix
\[
 a=\log2,\quad r=2^{-1/2},\quad
 \delta=(\log3-\log2)/8,\quad I=(-\delta,\delta).
\]
The Fourier convention is
\(\widehat h(\xi)=\int h(x)e^{-i\xi x}dx\), without the unitary factor. Write
\[
 H(h)=\|h\|_2^2,\quad
 v_h=\frac{U_{a/2}h-U_{-a/2}h}{\sqrt{2H(h)}},\quad
 W_h=\frac{(1-\cos(a\xi))|\widehat h|^2}{H(h)}.
 \tag{1}
\]
For support in the closed interval and a nonzero test, the two lobes are disjoint and
\[
 \|v_h\|_2=1,\qquad \int_{\mathbb R}W_h\,d\xi=2\pi.
 \tag{2}
\]
Indeed the autocorrelation at a is zero, while Plancherel gives the remaining mass. No regularity assumption beyond L2 is needed for (2). [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

For h4, the supplied coefficients are exactly those of \(\eta_4''-\eta_4/4\). Both moments \(\int h_4e^{\pm x/2}\) vanish by two integrations by parts: eta4 and its first derivative vanish at both endpoints. The zero extension of h4 is C1 and H2, not C-infinity. Section 3.7 supplies its actual form-domain extension, rather than silently changing `C_c^infinity(I)` into a different class. The norm formula in [H4] follows by integrating each monomial. [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

### 1.2 The two strengthened tails are valid

The elementary estimate
\[
 |J(\beta,\xi)|\le\int_0^1(-\log v)v^{-1/2}dv=4
 \tag{3}
\]
holds for every real xi. Together with the independently proved bound in Section 2 it gives
\[
 |t_2(\xi)|\le T=
 \frac1{2\pi}\left[4+\sum_{j\ge0}
 \min\{4,256\beta_j^{-1/2}(1+\log\beta_j)\}\right],
 \quad\beta_j=2\pi2^j.
 \tag{4}
\]
The tail of the displayed positive series is a geometric sum with a linear factor in j. `budget.Tstar` keeps it. Thus the recorded ball around 13.9371046604 is an upper-majorant computation; **13.937** by itself is not an outward-rounded bound. One may safely state the simpler recorded consequence \(T<14\). [ABSTRACT][PAPER; FINITE_CELL][ARB_INTERVAL for the supplied value]

If h and h' vanish at both endpoints and h is the given interior polynomial, integration by parts gives
\[
 |\widehat h(\xi)|\le B_3|\xi|^{-3}+B_4|\xi|^{-4},
\quad B_3=2|h''(\delta)|,
\quad B_4\ge2|h'''(\delta)|+\|h''''\|_{L^1(-\delta,\delta)}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Specifically, the third integration exposes the two endpoint values of h''; integrating the remaining h''' integral once more gives the two endpoint values of h''' and its interior derivative. Parity makes the two absolute endpoint values equal; no distributional fourth derivative of the zero extension is incorrectly integrated as an ordinary function.

Squaring the right side, using \(1-\cos\le2\), and integrating both frequency half-lines proves exactly
\[
 \mu_X:=\int_{|\xi|>X}W_h
 \le\frac4H\left(
 \frac{B_3^2}{5X^5}+\frac{2B_3B_4}{6X^6}+\frac{B_4^2}{7X^7}\right).
 \tag{6}
\]
The code bounds the L1 norm in B4 by the sum of absolute monomial integrals. Its reported 8.03769798335e10 is therefore a permissible **majorant**, not an independently computed equality to the optimal B4. This wording correction does not weaken the certificate. [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

### 1.3 Analytic quadrature: the actual holomorphic extension

The real-axis integrand must not be continued by writing a complex real part or a modulus square. For these real-even polynomial tests its correct extension is
\[
 -\frac{(1-\cos az)\widehat h(z)^2}{H}
 \bigl[\gamma_2(z)t_2^{[J]}(z)+\gamma_2(-z)t_2^{[J]}(-z)\bigr].
 \tag{7}
\]
On the real line this equals the evaluated expression, because the reflected factor is its complex conjugate. For a packet replace the square by the product of the two real-even transforms. A general complex test instead needs the reflected-conjugate entire function, not that real-even shortcut.

For s=1/2-iz, the defining Mellin integral is analytic when Re s>0, equivalently Im z>-1/2, by dominated differentiation on closed substrips. Its reflected factor is analytic for Im z<1/2. The gamma ratio and the Euler denominator have no poles in the open strip |Im z|<1/2; the relevant numerator-gamma poles and denominator-Euler zeros first meet its upper boundary, and reflection gives the lower boundary for the other factor. The test transforms and lobe factor are entire. Therefore (7) is holomorphic on that strip. [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

For a panel of width w and Bernstein parameter rho, the ellipse's semiaxes are
\[
 R_{\rm maj}=\frac w4(\rho+\rho^{-1}),\qquad
 R_{\rm min}=\frac w4(\rho-\rho^{-1}).
 \tag{8}
\]
The h4 run uses w=1/2 and rho=3. Its use of Rmaj for the imaginary bound is conservative: Rmaj=5/12<1/2, whereas the actual Rmin=1/3. The packet's later use of Rmin for the imaginary extent and Rmaj for the real extent is correct. Choosing a better rho after evaluating the same quadrature rule is legitimate: rho changes the proof of its error, not the rule or the test.

The estimates \(|\widehat h(z)|\le\|h\|_1e^{\delta|\Im z|}\) and
\[
 |J(\beta,\pm z)|\le(1/2-|\Im z|)^{-2}
\]
give the stated M. The gamma numerator and reciprocal-gamma bounds are applied on enclosing rectangles; the denominator obeys \(|b(z)|\ge1-r e^{aR_{\rm min}}>0\). The shifted-gamma fallback is a modulus bound, not a branch-changing approximation. Reflection makes the two gamma suprema on the symmetric rectangles equal; either valid enclosure is usable.

[ATAP, Theorem 8.2] gives interpolant error at most \(4M\rho^{-n}/(\rho-1)\). Integrating its uniform error over [-1,1] gives \(8M\rho^{-n}/(\rho-1)\); multiply by each panel's half-width and then by two for the two frequency half-lines. These are exactly the scaling factors in `cert.py`. The interval panel cover is complete for the main runs: X=2000 or 4000 and w=1/2 are exactly representable and the number of panels is integral. A non-dyadic cross-check's panel cover is not used as the proof of the main cover. [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

### 1.4 Large-beta formula and the series stopping rule

For 0<Re s<1,
\[
 \int_0^\infty v^{s-1}\cos(\beta v)dv
 =\beta^{-s}\Gamma(s)\cos(\pi s/2).
\]
Subtract the integral from 1 to infinity and differentiate in s. The result is
\[
 J(\beta,\xi)=\beta^{-s}\Gamma(s)
 \left[(\log\beta-\psi(s))\cos(\pi s/2)
                  +(\pi/2)\sin(\pi s/2)\right]+R_\beta(s),
 \tag{9}
\]
with \(s=1/2+i\xi\) and
\(R_\beta(s)=\int_1^\infty(\log v)v^{s-1}\cos(\beta v)dv\).
For the t2 evaluator substitute -xi. The improper integral and its differentiated version converge locally uniformly in the indicated strip after an integration by parts.

Let \(P_0=1,Q_0=0\),
\[
 P_{m+1}=(s-1-m)P_m,\qquad Q_{m+1}=(s-1-m)Q_m+P_m.
\]
Then
\[
 \frac{d^m}{dv^m}\bigl((\log v)v^{s-1}\bigr)
 =v^{s-1-m}(P_m\log v+Q_m).
\]
The boundary at infinity vanishes at each integration; the first boundary at 1 vanishes since log 1=0. At the real frequency nodes, Re s=1/2, the remainder after k integrations is bounded by
\[
 |E_k|\le\beta^{-k}
 \left(\frac{|P_k|}{(k-1/2)^2}+\frac{|Q_k|}{k-1/2}\right).
 \tag{10}
\]
The first nonzero boundary contribution is \(-\cos\beta/\beta^2\); the subsequent four-cycle of sine/cosine signs agrees with the implementation. On a complex ellipse the denominators would instead depend on Re s. The code does **not** use (10) as its ellipse bound: it uses the defining-integral majorant from Section 1.3. [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

There is a small but important repair to the series explanation. For complex s the terms are not an alternating real sequence. The valid estimate is an **absolute geometric tail**. At the actual stopping index k=2n, the undivided coefficient satisfies \(\beta^k/k!<1\). Since
\(k!\le((k+1)/2)^k\), this implies k+1>2 beta. Therefore the ratio of subsequent factorial coefficients is less than 1/4. The factors |s+2n| increase when Re s>0, so including their squared denominators cannot enlarge this ratio. Twice the first omitted term is safe. The same argument applies to the polynomial-transform cosine series. This proves the implemented tail rather than relying on the misleading alternating-series comment.

Float conversions used to choose a precision or one of several **already enclosed** asymptotic values do not remove its error ball. All selected values retain (10). Node construction at much higher precision prevents input-radius cancellation from invalidating the resulting useful widths; ball validity itself does not depend on a small width.

### 1.6 Packet: exact rank, correct order, and a remaining receipt issue

The identity
\[
 \eta_5=\eta_4-z^2\eta_4,\qquad h_5=h_4-h_{4z}
 \tag{13}
\]
is exact. The map \(\eta\mapsto\eta''-\eta/4\) is injective on polynomials, by comparison of the top degree. Thus the requested span has dimension three. Its redundant four-coordinate Gram and floor matrices have common kernel (1,-1,0,-1); an ordinary inverse of that four-coordinate Gram is not defined.

On a linearly independent packet define the **unnormalized** matrices
\[
 F_{ij}=-\int (1-\cos a\xi)\overline{\widehat h_i}\widehat h_j\ell_2\,d\xi,
\quad M_{ij}=-\int (1-\cos a\xi)\overline{\widehat h_i}\widehat h_j d_2\,d\xi.

Section 3 proves \(\mathbf M-\mathbf F\succeq0\), including all complex coefficient directions. Therefore a verified pencil bound \(\mathbf F\succeq c\mathbf H\) proves \(\mathfrak m(h)\ge c\) on its entire nonzero span. Positivity of three diagonal values alone would not do so. [FINITE_CELL][PAPER]

\[

where \(D_i=2\pi H_{ii}-\int_{-X}^X(1-\cos a\xi)|\widehat h_i|^2\) and its **upper** bound includes its own mass quadrature error. Both follow from Cauchy--Schwarz against a nonnegative measure. ### Theorem 1. A uniform constant 128, hence the requested 256

For beta>=1 and every real xi,
\[
 \boxed{|J(\beta,\xi)|\le128\beta^{-1/2}(1+\log\beta).}
 \tag{15}
\]
In particular [SF1, (31)] with constant 256 stands. The smaller constant is not inserted retroactively into the existing certificate. No constant 8 is claimed. [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

**Proof.** Substitute y=beta v and split the cosine into two exponentials. Apart from the exterior modulus beta^-1/2, each integral has amplitude
\(b(y)=y^{-1/2}\log(\beta/y)\) and phase \(\phi(y)=\pm y+\xi\log y\).
On 0<y<1 its absolute integral is at most \(2\log\beta+4\).

Partition [1,beta] into dyadic intervals [B,2B], truncating the last one. If |xi|<B/2 or |xi|>4B, the monotone derivative has modulus at least 1/2. Integration by parts bounds the integral of the unit-amplitude exponential on every subinterval by 8. Since b is decreasing, Stieltjes integration against that primitive bounds the weighted contribution by \(8B^{-1/2}\log\beta\). Summing these bounds gives at most
\(8(1-2^{-1/2})^{-1}\log\beta\).

The other intervals have B/2<=|xi|<=4B. There are at most four dyadic B in the interval [|xi|/4,2|xi|]. On each, \(|\phi''|\ge1/(8B)\), with constant sign. For a lower bound lambda on its magnitude, the set |phi'|<=sqrt(lambda) has length at most 2/sqrt(lambda); the two complementary intervals contribute at most 6/sqrt(lambda) by integration by parts. Thus every subinterval primitive is bounded by 8/sqrt(lambda). Multiplication by the decreasing amplitude gives at most \(16\sqrt2\log\beta\) per stationary interval, hence \(64\sqrt2\log\beta\) altogether.

Combining the estimates gives
\[
 |J|\le\beta^{-1/2}
 \left[4+\left(2+\frac8{1-2^{-1/2}}+64\sqrt2\right)\log\beta\right].
 \tag{16}
\]
The coefficient in parentheses is less than 128. For example sqrt(2)<10/7 bounds it by 2+28+640/7<128. Averaging the two exponentials adds no factor. This proves (15). QED.

The same argument without the logarithmic amplitude proves
\[
 |I(\beta,\xi)|\le128\beta^{-1/2}\quad(\beta\ge1),
 \qquad |I(\beta,\xi)|\le2\quad(\beta\ge0).
 \tag{17}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \|I(\beta\,\cdot,\xi)\|_{L^2(0,1)}^2
 \le\frac{4+128^2\log\beta}{\beta}\quad(\beta\ge1).
 \tag{18}
\]
Split that norm integral at 1/beta to verify (18). These deliberately nonsharp bounds are uniform in real xi and will close the source-domain convergence below.

For comparison, at xi=0 the full Mellin term in (9) has leading coefficient
\(\Gamma(1/2)\cos(\pi/4)=\sqrt{\pi/2}>1\) multiplying beta^-1/2 log beta; the tail in (9) is O(beta^-2). Thus the old uniform constant 1 is genuinely false, not simply unverified. This counterexample concerns that old bound, not the current 256 certificate. [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

## 3. Q3 -- complete tested source construction at cutoff one

This section is a new PAPER proof of the source identity on the requested class. It does not assume RESONANCE (6) in order to derive it. Ordinary Hilbert-space/Fourier facts are used, and the finite-Euler convention is fixed by [CCM23]. None of the proof uses a sign assertion about the Weil form.

### 3.1 Exact operators and strict contraction

Work first on the physical half-line with Lebesgue measure. Let
\[
 (F_\infty f)(u)=2\int_0^\infty\cos(2\pi uv)f(v)dv
\]
be the unitary cosine involution. Transport to the log line by the unitary
\((Vg)(u)=u^{-1/2}g(\log u)\). The cutoff P is multiplication by 1 on 0<u<1, or x<0 in log coordinates. E denotes zero extension from (0,1).

For p=2 put B=I-rU_a and
\[
 F=B(B^*)^{-1}F_\infty=(B^*)^{-1}F_\infty B^*,
 \quad Q=FPF,\quad R=I-P-Q,
 \quad S=\operatorname{proj}(\ker P\cap\ker Q).
 \tag{19}
\]
The identities follow from \(F_\infty U_a=U_{-a}F_\infty\). In particular F is a real self-adjoint unitary involution. Its norm-convergent expansion is
\[
 F=\left((1-r^2)\sum_{j\ge0}r^jU_{-ja}-rU_a\right)F_\infty.
 \tag{20}
\]
This is the literal finite-Euler operator; no truncated multiplier is declared unitary.

Set A=E*FE. Each compressed summand in (20) has a smooth cosine kernel on the finite square, hence is compact. Norm convergence makes A compact. If its norm were one, the compact self-adjoint operator would have an eigenvector with eigenvalue +1 or -1. Equality in the projection norm would imply that a nonzero cutoff-supported f and Ff were both cutoff-supported. Both B* and its inverse preserve this cutoff range. Applying B* would give a nonzero ordinary compactly supported function and its compactly supported cosine transform. The latter is entire and vanishes on an open real interval outside its support, hence vanishes identically; unitarity then contradicts nonzero f. Therefore
\[
 \alpha:=\|A\|<1,\qquad Z=(I-A^2)^{-1}\text{ is bounded}.
 \tag{21}
\]
No numerical value for 1-alpha is needed in this argument. [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

The finite-Euler source is also the Sonin-image model used by the parents: B and B^-1 preserve the positive log half-line and FB=BF_infinity. Therefore B carries the ordinary common-kernel space bijectively onto the common-kernel space in (19). It is bounded invertible, not generally unitary; the orthogonal projector is the parent Euler-Gram projector. This preserves the exact source rather than replacing the Sonin space by a surrogate.

### 3.2 Generalized waves, without an illegal L2 expansion

Use unitary Fourier waves
\[
 e_\xi(u)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}u^{-1/2+i\xi}.
\]
Their restrictions to (0,1) are **not L2**. The following notation will mean a constructed Mellin integral, not an application of an L2 operator to an inadmissible vector:
\[
 u(\xi)=A(e_\xi|_{(0,1)}),\qquad
 t(\xi)=\langle e_\xi,Ae_{-\xi}\rangle_{(0,1)}.
\]
The physical expansion of (20) has coefficients
\[
 \beta_{-1}=\pi,\ c_{-1}=-1/2,\qquad
 \beta_j=2\pi2^j,\ c_j=1/2\quad(j\ge0).
\]
Define them rigorously by
\[
 u(\xi)(u)=\sqrt{2/\pi}\sum_{j\ge-1}c_jI(\beta_j u,\xi),
 \quad t(\xi)=\frac1\pi\sum_{j\ge-1}c_jJ(\beta_j,-\xi).
 \tag{22}
\]
The first series converges in L2(0,1), uniformly in real xi, by (18) and the summability of \(2^{-j/2}\sqrt{1+j}\). The second converges uniformly by (15) and the summability of \(2^{-j/2}(1+j)\). Each finite term is continuous in xi. Thus u is a continuous L2-valued function and t is continuous; both are bounded.

For a single cosine summand, the formula for t follows from the absolutely convergent double integral and
\[
 \int_0^1\int_0^1 G(uv)\,du\,dv
       =\int_0^1(-\log w)G(w)\,dw.
\]
For the vector use its ordinary absolutely convergent integral in the second physical variable. Infinite Euler sums are passed through **these convergent scalar/vector series**, not through an absolute pointwise sum of the lacunary cosine kernel. That latter sum need not be a function.

For each fixed beta, the Mellin integrals tend to zero as |xi| tends to infinity by the Riemann--Lebesgue lemma after v=exp(-x); the vector version follows by domination in u. Uniformly convergent tails in (22) consequently show
\[
 u(\xi)\longrightarrow0\text{ in L2},\qquad t(\xi)\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{23}
\]
A rate is not necessary for the present domain proof. [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

Direct Mellin transformation of the cosine kernel gives, distributionally,
\[
 Fe_\xi=\gamma(\xi)e_{-\xi},\quad
 \gamma(\xi)=\pi^{-i\xi}
 \frac{\Gamma(1/4+i\xi/2)}{\Gamma(1/4-i\xi/2)}
 \frac{1-re^{ia\xi}}{1-re^{-ia\xi}},\quad |\gamma|=1.
 \tag{24}
\]
For the ordinary factor, integrate the cosine against u^-1/2+i xi with exponential damping and remove the damping; duplication and reflection reduce it to the stated ratio. Norm-convergent shifts give the Euler factor. This distribution identity on Schwartz tests does not assert that e_xi is a Hilbert-space vector.

### 3.3 A trace-class lemma with an actual domain

Write C_b for the Fourier multiplier b, and let P be the negative-half-line projection. If b is Schwartz, then [P,C_b] is trace class. A half-line Hankel operator with a Schwartz kernel k(x+y), on either same-sign quadrant, is also trace class.

Here is a direct justification sufficient for both assertions. On opposite-sign quadrants the commutator kernel is, up to sign, a Schwartz function of the sum of the two positive distances from zero. Partition each positive axis by a smooth partition into intervals of bounded length. On each rectangle extend the localized smooth kernel slightly and expand it in a Fourier series. Two derivatives in each variable bound coefficients by a constant times
\[
 (1+j+k)^{-N}(1+n^2)^{-1}(1+m^2)^{-1},
\]
where j,k index the physical rectangles. The cutoff extensions have uniformly bounded derivatives. Each Fourier term is a rank-one operator with a uniformly bounded product of L2 norms. Summing the coefficient absolute values over n,m and then j,k, with N>2, gives a nuclear decomposition with finite trace norm. The same argument treats the Hankel kernel. Restriction to the quadrants is by orthogonal projections and cannot increase trace norm. The estimates are controlled by finitely many Schwartz seminorms. This proves the lemma, including the endpoint blocks; it is the local content of [CC20, Appendix D]. [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

If v is compact smooth, \(\widehat v\,m\) is Schwartz for the source multiplier
\(m(\xi)=\gamma(-\xi)\): derivatives of the gamma phase have at most polynomial/logarithmic growth on the real line, and the fixed Euler denominators are separated from zero. Therefore
\[
 T_vPFP=[T_v,P]FP+PT_vFP
 \tag{25}
\]
is trace class. The second term is a same-half-line Hankel operator, since F=C_m times reflection. For each shifted ordinary Fourier term in (20), its trace norm in (25) grows at most polynomially in |j|: modulation differentiations only introduce powers of j in the Schwartz seminorms. The coefficients r^j make this series converge in trace norm.

We will also use that T_vEA is Hilbert--Schmidt. For a finite Euler partial sum A_J its Fourier-coordinate kernel is
\(\widehat v(\xi)\langle u_J(\xi),\cdot\rangle\).
The uniform L2-valued convergence and boundedness in (22), together with \(\int|\widehat v|^2<\infty\), make these kernels converge in Hilbert--Schmidt norm. Since A_J also converges to A in operator norm, the limit is the actual T_vEA. The same reasoning, using (24), applies to T_vFEA. No eigen-expansion of e_xi is used.

### 3.4 The arithmetic trace, with no subtraction of infinite traces

In log Fourier coordinates, F=C_m times reflection, so
\[
 R=C_mPC_m^*-P,\qquad m'/m=-i q_2,
\]
\[
 q_2(\xi)=\Re\psi(1/4+i\xi/2)-\log\pi
               -2a\sum_{j\ge1}r^j\cos(ja\xi).
 \tag{26}
\]
For Schwartz a1,b1,
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(C_{a_1}[P,C_{b_1}])
             =\frac{i}{2\pi}\int a_1'(\xi)b_1(\xi)\,d\xi.
 \tag{27}
\]
To see this, let k_a,k_b be the inverse multiplier kernels. The spatial trace integral is
\(\int h k_a(h)k_b(-h)dh\): indeed
\(\int[1_{x-h<0}-1_{x<0}]dx=h\).
It is absolutely convergent. Fourier inversion gives (27). The trace-class lemma makes this a trace identity rather than a formal diagonal integral.

Set a1=hat(v)m, b1=overline(hat(v)m), a0=hat(v), b0=overline(hat(v)). Their multiplier products agree. Consequently
\[
 T_vRT_v^*=C_{a_1}[P,C_{b_1}]-C_{a_0}[P,C_{b_0}]
\]
is trace class, and (27) gives
\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{Tr}(T_vRT_v^*)
             =\frac1{2\pi}\int|\widehat v(\xi)|^2q_2(\xi)d\xi.}
 \tag{28}
\]
The terms being subtracted in this displayed commutator expression are trace class. We have not subtracted the separate infinite traces of P and Q.

Finally, digamma's integral formula gives
\[
 q_\infty(\xi)=2\int_0^\infty
 \frac{e^{-t/2}}{1-e^{-2t}}(1-\cos\xi t)dt-c_A,
 \quad c_A=\gamma_E+\log(8\pi)+\pi/2.
\]
Parseval identifies its tested value with the archimedean difference energy minus c_A||v||². Each cosine in (26) gives exactly \(-2ar^j C_v(ja)\), with \(C_v(t)=\Re\int\bar v(x)v(x+t)dx\). This is the literal geometric L2 functional of the request. On the minus class the two pole moments vanish, its autocorrelation diameter is a+2delta<log3, and the only nonzero prime-power atom is 2. Thus (28) equals L2(v), and L2(v) equals the full geometric Weil value for these tests. No statement about zeta zeros has been used. [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

### 3.5 Gram inversion and the density formula

Let W=(E,FE). Then
\[
 W^*W=\begin{pmatrix}I&A\\A&I\end{pmatrix}
\]
is boundedly invertible by (21), so ran W is closed and its orthogonal projection is W(W*W)^-1W*. Define D=S-R. Direct multiplication gives
\[
 \boxed{D=W\begin{pmatrix}-A^2Z&AZ\\AZ&-A^2Z\end{pmatrix}W^*.}
 \tag{29}
\]
Split AZ=A+A³Z. The linear part is PQ+QP. Its tested trace is defined by (25); the other terms factor through the Hilbert--Schmidt maps T_vEA and T_vFEA. Thus T_vDT_v* is trace class.

For a finite Euler term in the linear part, its Fourier diagonal is computed by the absolutely convergent double Mellin integral in Section 3.2. Formula (24) on the external F gives the two cross terms gamma t and its conjugate. Summation in trace norm, justified after (25), and uniform convergence of the t-series give
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(T_v(PQ+QP)T_v^*)
       =\int|\widehat v(\xi)|^2\,2\Re(\gamma(\xi)t(\xi))d\xi.
 \tag{30}
\]
One explicit way to justify the diagonal operation is to insert a finite Fourier-band projection on both sides first. Each finite-kernel diagonal is continuous on that band and its double Mellin integral is absolutely convergent. Its trace is the integral of that diagonal: finite-rank step-function projections average the continuous kernel on shrinking frequency squares, while trace-class convergence identifies the same limit with the operator trace. Remove the band projection using trace-norm convergence. The integrals converge by the uniform bound for t times |hat(v)|². This specifies the regularization; it does not attach an untested trace to PQ.

For the quadratic terms the Hilbert--Schmidt kernels already constructed give, by the usual trace of a product of Hilbert--Schmidt operators,
\[
 \boxed{\begin{split}
 d_2(\xi)={}&2\Re\!\left\{\gamma(\xi)
    \left[t(\xi)+\langle u(\xi),AZ\overline{u(\xi)}\rangle\right]\right\}\\
 &-2\langle u(\xi),Zu(\xi)\rangle,\\
 \operatorname{Tr}(T_vDT_v^*)&=\int|\widehat v(\xi)|^2d_2(\xi)d\xi.
 \end{split}}
 \tag{31}
\]
Reality of A gives u(-xi)=overline(u(xi)); |gamma|=1 makes the two diagonal quadratic terms equal. Bounded Z and the uniform bounds in (22) dominate every scalar integral. The right side of (31) is continuous and bounded. Its limit at both frequency infinities is zero by (23).

Because S=R+D, equations (28)--(31) prove that T_vST_v* is positive trace class. Therefore its trace really is the source Sonin quantity n2(v), and
\[
 \boxed{n_2(v)-L_2(v)=\operatorname{Tr}(T_vDT_v^*)
                         =\int|\widehat v|^2d_2.}
 \tag{32}
\]
This establishes the requested source identity and its tested domain, including all complex smooth minus tests. It is not conditional on a numerical convergence experiment. The pointwise continuous density representative is the one in (31). Positive tested traces imply its Sonin density k2=q2/(2pi)+d2 is nonnegative almost everywhere, hence everywhere for its continuous representative. [ABSTRACT][PAPER]

 D=P+Q-I+S\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D+D^2=PQ+QP.

This follows by expanding the square and using (P+Q)S=0. The tested operators in (33) are trace class by the preceding construction. Therefore

 \boxed{\mathfrak m(h)=L_2(v_h)-n_2(v_h)

In particular the packet numerator difference is positive semidefinite. This also identifies the correct sign without an expansion of the non-L2 wave in an eigenbasis.

Changing the square-root phase changes neither quadratic value. This recovers [SF1]'s floor lemma with all of its analytic premises now supplied above.

### 4.2 The source compact operator and why multiplier signs do not decide it

 \tag{38}

The multiplier is bounded, real, even and vanishes at infinity by Section 3.2. ### 4.4 Explicit absolute complement bounds, with their exact limitation

Here are constants for a real test rather than a free “tail small” assumption. Let V_N be span of P00 applied to monomials of degrees 0,...,N-1, and Pi_N its exact orthogonal projection.  s_N(X)=\min\{1,\sqrt{2\delta X/\pi}\,b_N(X)\}.

 |J(\beta,\xi)|\le C_\beta/\xi^2\quad(\xi\ne0).
