# Finite full-parity Suzuki W diagnostics

Status: **FINITE_GALERKIN_PROTOTYPE_NOT_SUZUKI_THEOREM**. Both ζ and Davenport–Heilbronn were computed for every integer m=20,…,240 (442 source matrices and shifted solves). The carrier contains the constant and both cosine and sine modes through m, on [−log(m)/2,log(m)/2]. Eight representative m=20,40,60,80,120,160,200,240 have full W diagnostics.

The physical-L² pencil is A−λI. The loads are the projections of exp(+x) and exp(−x); the latter profile is obtained by reflection. Both vectors share unit T-energy norm. Main shifts are λ=−1 for ζ and one below the full **finite** Ritz minimum for DH. Their finite gaps are approximately at least one. This does not verify λ<λ_a for the continuum operator. A conservative source lower bound and a safely lower proposed shift are recorded in every scalar row, but that different shift was not substituted silently into the reported experiment.

Both pole terms are retained: positive even rank one and negative odd rank one for ζ; DH has no pole term. The odd block and odd exponential load were independently reviewed against the physical sine basis. At m20, independent 80-digit GL128 full-parity source solves agree with float profiles to relative L² errors 2.54e−15 (ζ), 9.08e−16 (DH). This is a bounded consistency check, not high-precision validation of all 442 cells. Tiny ζ Ritz signs at approximately 1e−13 are roundoff-scale and carry no negativity verdict.

The H¹ endpoint-zero tests are odd sine modes and even cosine-minus-endpoint-constant modes, n=1,2,3. Both each test and its derivative lie in the full carrier, so their source form pairings use the actual finite source matrix without an omitted cross term. For v+, q_T(u′+u,v+)=0 is tested; the largest defect is 2.45e−15. The reversed sign is retained as a failing control. Passing these tests follows algebraically from the finite solve and the exact exponential-load integration by parts; it is an internal consistency/sign control, not independent missing-mode control. These finitely many H¹ tests do not establish the adjoint equation on its complete continuum domain.

W uses the plus Fourier exponent, v+ for exp(+x), and θ=2πj/16. The exact origin identity is Wθ(0)=i E(exp(iθ)−1). Thus θ=0 supplies the historical v1 target's origin zero, whereas θ=π is compatible with the nonzero v3 origin. An independent T=I control verifies the exact W formula at 60 digits: it already has an arithmetic-free real lattice of roots with spacing 2π/log(m). Consequently real roots or their coarse density alone are not evidence of ζ identification.

The historical-v1 target is z²ξ(s)/ξ′(s); current-v3 is ξ(s)/(ξ(s)+ξ′(s)), s=1/2−iz. DH uses its own completed Dirichlet function in both ratios. Gamma factors cancel analytically. Removable ξ points s=0,1 and the cancelled DH Hurwitz pole at s=1 are evaluated explicitly. No point is discarded for convenience.

For each target, a constant gauge is anchored at z=i/4. Among sixteen θ values, the first 21 positive real coordinates select the smallest calibration RMS error; the remaining 20 coordinates are held out, on each of Im z=.05,.25,.5. Negative coordinates are also evaluated, using target conjugation and independent W evaluation. The requested real range 0…0.8Ω expands with m, so it is a stronger domain test than fixed-compact convergence. A separate fixed compact 0≤Re z≤20 with the same three heights, calibration split, and negative-reflection checks is recorded in `selected_gauges_fixed20.csv`. `selected_gauges.csv` contains the resulting errors. This restricted constant-gauge family is not the arbitrary zero-free analytic gauge of Suzuki's existential statement; failure of these fits does not refute that statement. Conversely fitting log(target/W) pointwise would provide no independent evidence.

`real_W_roots.csv` reports every sign-change root found on a real grid of maximum spacing .025 and refined by Brent. These are finite float roots, not a certified complete zero set: tangencies and paired roots between grid points are not excluded. The nearest ξ′ reference is independently selected for each root, with no forced one-to-one matching. The supplied ξ′ reference list is itself a scoped numerical list. Its use for DH is explicitly labelled as a ζ reference, not the DH derivative's own zeros.

At the DH off-line zero z≈85.6993484854+0.308517182457i, m240 θ=π gives |W(z)|≈2.59514644. For current-v3, the constant-gauge relative maximum on radius .1,.03,.01 circles is 28.05,62.58,165.30. At radii .001,.0001,.00001 it becomes 1557.36,15479.85,154704.93; radius times error tends toward 1.547. Historical-v1 has a much smaller anchored scale: the same tiny radii yield 1.281,3.817,29.190, revealing its asymptotic inverse-radius behavior only later. A nonvanishing analytic multiplier cannot create the missing target zero at this fixed finite m. This is not a theorem about a sequence of gauges or a-limit convergence. Center coordinates were rounded to float for W; `dh_circle.csv` reports the resulting nonzero numerical target residual at the nominal center rather than declaring it exactly zero numerically.

On the fixed compact, ζ heldout RMS changes from 1.00183 to 1.01266 (v1) and 1.88830 to 2.00998 (v3) between m20 and m240. DH changes from 1.00167 to .99767 (v1) and 1.83144 to 2.54909 (v3). Thus this particular constant-gauge diagnostic does not improve toward zero even on a fixed compact.

The first unpaid mathematical steps are the continuum shift/domain requirements, convergence of finite deficiency vectors in the appropriate energy space, control of the analytic gauges, and the relevant limiting topology. Ordinary relative errors at target zeros, or ordinary uniform errors on compacts containing target poles, are undefined. The reported grids stay in the upper half-plane and the circle rows describe their stated finite punctured sets.
