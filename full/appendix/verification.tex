\paragraph{Elementary inequalities used in A3.}
The A3 floor proof (Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-arch-floor}) uses only fixed constant bounds and
elementary inequalities. For audit convenience we record the ones invoked explicitly in
Section~\ref{subsec:a3-symbol-floor}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.2em]
  \item $\pi<\tfrac{22}{7}$ and $\pi>3$ (classical rational bounds).
  \item $\pi>\tfrac{333}{106}$ (classical lower bound; used for the exponentials in Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-arch-floor}).
  \item $\log(3/2)<\tfrac{5}{12}$ from the alternating series
  $\log(1+x)=x-\tfrac{x^2}{2}+\tfrac{x^3}{3}-\tfrac{x^4}{4}+\cdots$ at $x=\tfrac12$.
  \item $\log(5/2)<1$ since $e>1+1+\tfrac12+\tfrac16+\tfrac{1}{24}=\tfrac{65}{24}>2.5$.
  \item $e<3$ from the series $e=\sum_{k\ge0}\tfrac{1}{k!}$ and a tail bound. Using the Taylor remainder at $x=\tfrac35$ gives
  \(
    e^{3/5}
    \le \sum_{j=0}^{4}\frac{(3/5)^j}{j!}+\frac{3}{120}\Big(\frac{3}{5}\Big)^{5}
    <\frac{50}{27},
  \)
  hence $e^{-3/5}>\tfrac{27}{50}$.
  \item With $x_0=\frac{665334}{280900}$ one has
  \(
    e^{x_0}\ge \sum_{j=0}^{5}\frac{x_0^j}{j!}>10,
  \)
  which implies $e^{6\pi^2/25}>10$, so
  \(w_{B_{\min}}(1)\le \tfrac{1}{15}\) and \(w_{B_{\min}}(2)\le \tfrac{1}{30000}\) in Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-arch-floor}.
  \item For $0\le x\le 1$, $e^x\le 1+x+x^2$ (apply $f(x)=1+x+x^2-e^x$ with
  $f(0)=f'(0)=0$ and $f'(1)=3-e>0$). Hence $e^{1/144}\le 1+\tfrac{1}{144}+\tfrac{1}{20736}<\tfrac{1007}{1000}$.
  \item $\gamma>0.5772$ (Euler's constant lower bound; see standard tables in
  \cite{Titchmarsh1986} or \cite{NISTDLMF}).
\end{itemize}
