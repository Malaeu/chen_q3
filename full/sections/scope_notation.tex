\section{Positioning and Scope}\label{sec:scope}

This work introduces a quantitative, modular operator framework for the Weil criterion. The present draft closes the analytic modules \textup{(T0)}, \textup{(A1$'$)}, \textup{(A2)}, \textup{(A3)}, and \textup{(RKHS)}, but it does \emph{not} yet close the support/closure gates needed to pass from those modules to unconditional Weil positivity on the full cone. The scope and boundaries are as follows.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What this is:} A unified analytic program with explicit constants (symbol modulus, RKHS cap, node spacing, tail control) whose current output is a typed conditional route to Weil positivity.
  \item \textbf{What this is not:} This draft does not yet claim that the closure chain is fully closed. The unresolved gates are isolated explicitly in Section~\ref{sec:Main-closure}.
  \item \textbf{Modularity:} Local improvements in the Archimedean symbol, the RKHS cap, or the support upgrade feed only the corresponding gate and do not change the rest of the chain.
  \item \textbf{Test class:} For each $K>0$ we distinguish
  \[
    R_K := C^+_{\mathrm{even}}([-K,K])
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    W_K := \bigl\{\Phi\in C_c(\mathbb R): \operatorname{supp}\Phi\subset[-K,K],\ \Phi\ \text{even},\ \Phi\ge 0\bigr\}.
  \]
  The global Weil cone is
  \[
    \mathcal W := \bigcup_{K>0} W_K.
  \]
  The density theorem A1$'$ is currently a theorem on the restriction cone $R_K$, while A2 and the LF route live on the admissible support cone $W_K$. The future common admissible generator family is denoted by $G_K\subseteq W_K$.
  \item \textbf{Verification path:} Sections~\ref{sec:T0}--\ref{sec:rkhs-prime-cap} supply the fully written analytic modules. Section~\ref{sec:Main-closure} packages the unresolved gates. Appendix~\ref{app:legacy-branch} keeps legacy branches as read-only provenance only.
  \item \textbf{Computation:} Symbol scans, ATP logs, and certificate tables are reproducibility aids only; they do not enter the logical core of the active proof chain.
\end{itemize}

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Bridge summary.} We split $Q^\star(t;\Phi_{B,t})$ via the Rayleigh identification into a Toeplitz block
and a Rayleigh prime block, i.e., $T_M[P_A](t,B,M)$ versus $T_P^{\mathrm{Ray}}(t,M)$.
The symbol barrier yields $\lambda_{\min}(T_M[P_A])\ge c_* - C\,\omega_{P_A}(1/(2M))$, where
$c_* = \tfrac{11}{10}$ is the \textbf{uniform Archimedean floor} from Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-arch-floor}
(for $t=t_{\mathrm{crit}}=\tfrac{3}{20}=0.15$, $B_{\min}=3$), valid on the full circle $\TT$ and independent of $K$.
The prime norm is bounded in the Arch-induced RKHS by the single-scale cap
\[
  \|T_P^{\mathrm{RKHS}}(t_{\mathrm{crit}})\|\le \rho(t_{\mathrm{crit}})\le c_*/4,
\]
at the bridge scale $t_{\mathrm{crit}}=3/20$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:uniform-prime-cap}). A stronger auxiliary cap at $t=1$ is recorded in Corollary~\ref{cor:pcu-uniform}. Thus
\[
  \lambda_{\min}(T_M[P_A]-T_P^{\mathrm{Ray}}(t,M))\ \ge\ \min P_A\ -\ C\,\omega_{P_A}(1/(2M))\ -\ \rho(t),
\]
closing the A3/RKHS analytic bridge. What remains is to feed this centered positivity mechanism into the correct admissible closure gates.

\section{Gate Contract}\label{sec:global-hyp}

For reference we collect the live proof contract used in the closure section.

\begin{description}
  \item[(T0)] Guinand--Weil normalization of $Q^\star(t;\cdot)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:T0-GW}).
  \item[(G0)] Domain/type repair: explicitly distinguish the restriction cone $R_K$, the admissible support cone $W_K$, and the future generator family $G_K$.
  \item[(G1)] Support upgrade: either density inside admissible $W_K$, or a restriction-to-support replacement theorem with explicit $Q^\star$-error control.
  \item[(G2)] Common family: fix one exact admissible generator family $G_K\subseteq W_K$ implementing the `G1` route.
  \item[(G3)] Positivity on that same family $G_K$.
  \item[(G4)] Compact closure on each $W_K$ using `A2` continuity and the exact family from `G2/G3`.
  \item[(G5)] LF lift from all $W_K$ to the global Weil cone $\mathcal W$.
  \item[(G6)] Weil linkage: positivity on $\mathcal W$ implies RH.
\end{description}

The current manuscript establishes the analytic modules behind these gates:

\begin{itemize}
  \item `T0` in Section~\ref{sec:T0};
  \item `A1$'$` as density on $R_K$ in Section~\ref{sec:A1};
  \item `A2` as continuity on admissible compact tests in Section~\ref{sec:A2};
  \item `A3` and `RKHS` as the centered positivity mechanism in Sections~\ref{sec:A3} and \ref{sec:RKHS}.
\end{itemize}

What remains unresolved in the present draft are exactly `G1`, `G2`, and `G3`; Section~\ref{sec:Main-closure} packages `G4` and `G5` conditionally on them, and Section~\ref{sec:Weil-linkage} supplies `G6`.

\section{Notation and Conventions}\label{sec:notation}

On the frequency axis we write $\xi=\eta/(2\pi)$. In the Toeplitz bridge we work on the unit circle
\(\TT=\RR/\ZZ\) with fundamental domain $[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12]$. The Archimedean density is
\[
  a(\xi)=\log\pi-\Re\psi\Big(\tfrac14+i\pi\xi\Big),\qquad a_*(\xi)=2\pi\,a(\xi),
\]
and prime nodes are at $\xi_n=\tfrac{\log n}{2\pi}$ with symmetric placement $\pm\xi_n$.  We distinguish two weight conventions:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  w_Q(n) &= \frac{2\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt{n}}
  &&\text{(the one-sided weight inside $Q^\ast(t;\Phi)$)}, \\
  w_{\mathrm{RKHS}}(n) &= \frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt{n}}
  &&\text{(the operator weight in the RKHS analysis)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Evenization lets us pass freely between them: doubling $w_{\mathrm{RKHS}}$ on $\xi_n>0$ gives $w_Q$, while placing both $\pm\xi_n$ leaves $w_{\mathrm{RKHS}}$ unchanged. All RKHS and operator bounds below use $w_{\mathrm{RKHS}}$; we abbreviate $w_{\max}:=\sup_n w_{\mathrm{RKHS}}(n)\le 2/e$. Throughout we use
\[
  Q^\star(t;\Phi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}} a_*(\xi)\,\Phi(\xi)\,d\xi\;-\;\sum_{n\ge2} w_Q(n)\,\Phi(\xi_n),
\]
with the bridge scale fixed at $t=t_{\mathrm{crit}}$; Section~\ref{sec:T0} records the exact crosswalk to the Guinand--Weil form.  (We call $Q^\star$ “quadratic” only because $\Phi=g*g^{\vee}$; as a functional of $\Phi$ it is linear.) Notational summaries and parameter tables are collected in Appendix~\ref{app:notation}.

\subsection{Contemporary Context and Inspiration}

This work was inspired by several recent developments in analytic number theory, computational complexity, and mathematical logic:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Analytic criteria.} Li's positivity sequence~\cite{Li1997} and the Jensen polynomial programme of Griffin--Ono--Rolen--Zagier~\cite{GriffinOnoRolenZagier2019} give logically equivalent restatements of RH; both inspire our insistence on keeping every cone generator and Lipschitz bound explicit.

  \item \textbf{Zero-density breakthroughs.} The new Dirichlet-polynomial bounds of Guth and Maynard~\cite{GuthMaynard2024} illustrate how much can be gained by encoding the zeta problem as a spectral estimate, a viewpoint we adopt through the Toeplitz bridge.

  \item \textbf{Near-miss invariants.} Rodgers and Tao's work on the de Bruijn--Newman constant~\cite{rodgers2020debruijn} shows that RH may be ``barely true'', motivating a watchdog table that certifies every slack we introduce along the chain.

  \item \textbf{Geometric and noncommutative ideas.} Fesenko's two-dimensional adelic programme~\cite{Fesenko2008} and the Connes--Marcolli noncommutative approach~\cite{ConnesMarcolli2008} highlight how positivity hinges on careful operator factorizations, reinforcing our choice to stay within verifiable Toeplitz/RKHS settings.

  \item \textbf{Physical operator heuristics.} PT-symmetric constructions such as Bender--Brody--M\"uller~\cite{BenderBrodyMuller2017} keep the Hilbert--P\'olya dream alive; our framework aims to supply the missing rigorous operator inequalities.

  \item \textbf{Geometric flows and smoothing.} Perelman's Ricci-flow programme~\cite{Perelman2002,Perelman2003} shows how parabolic averaging can enforce global structure; we mirror that philosophy by pairing Fej\'er kernels with heat-flow smoothing in the Toeplitz bridge.

  \item \textbf{Programmatic limit architectures.} Deng--Hani--Ma~\cite{DengHaniMa2025Hilbert6} present a two-step limiting program (Newtonian dynamics $\to$ Boltzmann $\to$ fluid equations) that highlights how long-time control stabilizes successive limits; our compact-by-compact transfer plays an analogous ``bridge'' role for the Weil criterion.

  \item \textbf{Trace-formula-to-gap pipelines.} Anantharaman--Monk~\cite{AnantharamanMonk2025FR} use length-spectrum data and trace-formula technology to obtain asymptotic spectral gaps for random hyperbolic surfaces, a context that reinforces why spectral/trace language is a natural narrative for Weil-criterion positivity (here ``Weil--Petersson'' is unrelated to the Weil criterion).

  \item \textbf{Massive computations.} Platt and Trudgian's verification of RH up to $3\cdot10^{12}$~\cite{PlattTrudgian2021}, together with surveys like Conrey's~\cite{Conrey2003}, emphasise the need for transparent, audit-friendly proofs rather than ever-larger numerics.

  \item \textbf{Cautionary analyses.} Cairo's audit of proposed counterexamples~\cite{cairo2025counterexample} underlines how fragile heuristic arguments can be; we therefore keep every analytic assumption explicit and machine-checkable.
\end{itemize}

\noindent While these works influenced our methodology, our approach is fundamentally distinct: we construct a self-contained, verifiable chain from Toeplitz positivity to Weil positivity, with all critical steps amenable to formal verification.
