# Literal CCM ground spectra

`run.py` builds the complete literal source at two independently rebuilt precisions for each integer m. It uses `../inputs/analytic_source.py`, whose archimedean digamma evaluation was independently compared with the pinned quadrature source and the separate even/odd quadrature implementation by the parent run. All signed finite atoms, endpoint tails, pole terms and planted pair are retained. This run makes no positivity or RH assumption and never substitutes a zero Gram matrix.

The native `loweig.c` solver reads the real symmetric source, performs MPFR Householder tridiagonalization, locates the two lowest eigenvalues by diagnostic Sturm bisection, obtains two eigenvectors by shifted tridiagonal inverse iteration, and applies the saved reflectors. The lowest two eigenvalues from both parity blocks determine the global winner and full simplicity gap. Arithmetic is round-to-nearest, not interval arithmetic: bisection widths and inertia ordering are diagnostics of the computed tridiagonal, not certified enclosures of the original functional. Native known-spectrum controls and an independent code audit live in `../review/`.

The standard source precision is 6m+256 bits, replayed at 128 additional bits. Zeta blocks use those complete precisions. A block whose double-precision source has a macroscopic isolated negative ground is only a candidate for the cheaper 512/640-bit eigensolver; the final ground is still determined from MPFR eigenpairs, residuals, both parity blocks and the complete source replay. All worlds at the same m retain the same complete source arithmetic precision. A cheap candidate test therefore does not select the ground or infer positivity.

Each atomic `vectors/{world}_m{m}.json` contains preferred and lower-precision even/odd vectors, both low eigenvalues in each block, gaps, source/solver precision, exact source hashes, residual norms, orthogonality and sign-aligned vector replay changes. Coefficients use the literal source convention: ψ0=1/√L, ψn=√(2/L)(−1)^n cos(2πnt/L); the odd basis is √(2/L)(−1)^n sin(2πnt/L). Odd arrays have length m indexed by n=1,...,m. Both unit eigenvectors are oriented with their largest absolute coordinate positive.

A resolved flag requires same-parity gap dominance over empirical error diagnostics, stable preferred/lower ground vectors and orthogonality of the two numerical eigenvectors; the merged global gap must also dominate the errors. No vector is claimed as a unique global ground if these checks fail. Source replay differences, high-vector error estimates, and Fourier error estimates are empirical diagnostics, not rigorous source bounds. In particular a worst-case L² perturbation estimate can be too pessimistic for the very small Fourier tails: actual different-vector Fourier-zero replay is performed by the separate roots worker.

`shard.py 0` and `shard.py 1` stream the even and odd integer m subsets independently. `summarize.py` writes `spectra.csv` and a completeness manifest requiring all 855 world cells (285 integer values × three worlds). A finite computed grid is evidence and does not establish an infinite-m theorem.

For macroscopic negative grounds the second eigenvalue can remain in an unresolved near-zero cluster at the cheaper solver precision. Its supplied absolute error and the CSV `lambda_even1_sign_resolved`/`lambda_odd1_sign_resolved` columns distinguish that situation. The ground eigenvalue and the difference λ1−λ0 can still be well resolved; no uniqueness assertion is made for the second eigenvector.

The bulk run switched from decimal to exact hexadecimal source serialization after six actual source blocks produced bit-identical eigenpairs with either format (`hex_input_validation.json`). The native parser accepts both formats. Each cell retains its actual C producer hash; the corresponding source snapshots are archived under `producers/`. The source matrix and spectral arithmetic are identical across this transport-only optimization.

The parent run also validated the source at the scale needed for the smallest eigenvalue: m=300, both parities, all worlds, against independent pinned-source quadrature at 2400 bits, eight panels and Gauss order 768. The preferred 2184-bit analytic source differed by at most 2.6304×10⁻⁶⁵⁷ per entry, while the 2400-bit analytic/quadrature comparison differed by at most 2.4615×10⁻⁷⁰⁰. The receipt is `../inputs/validation_ground_scale_m300.json`. These empirical source differences are far below λ0≈2.45×10⁻⁵⁰⁵; they remain distinct from interval enclosures.

A bounded separate-multiply/add performance probe at m=206 passed its numerical comparisons but achieved only 1.023× end-to-end speedup (`unfused_probe.json`), below the preselected 1.5× adoption threshold. It was not adopted; all production cells use the reviewed fused-multiply/add solver.

After `shifted_low_final_review.json` passed, later cells use an additional speedup confined to LOW zeta validation. The preferred HIGH source still receives the complete ordered native lowest-two eigensolve. The independently rebuilt LOW source is rounded to its actual solver precision; measured LOW/HIGH point-matrix differences and HIGH gap diagnostics establish the corresponding LOW ground before one shifted real-Arb solve refines it from the HIGH seed. The solver context is explicitly LOW_SOLVER_BITS+72. LOW λ1 and LOW v1 are **not computed** in this mode: their actual replay fields are null, with an explicitly labeled empirical point-matrix Weyl interval for LOW λ1. Any refinement rejection falls back to the existing full native LOW block solve and records the reason. Preferred λ0, λ1, both parity vectors, all gaps and global parity continue to come from the full native HIGH solve.

The reviewed helper retains its staging filename `shifted_low_candidate.py`; `shifted_adoption_manifest.json` binds its approved hash, the production driver hash and the independent review receipt. The m=300 end-to-end LOW phase (conversion, shifted solve and checks) took 9.346 CPU seconds against 24.447 native LOW CPU seconds, a 2.616× block speedup. Source construction was shared and excluded from both LOW phase comparisons. All 690 world files complete at adoption retained their raw SHA256 hashes; source, HIGH solver and native executable were unchanged. LOW refinement residuals and point-matrix Weyl estimates are numerical diagnostics, not rigorous enclosures after decimal conversion.

Final closure: all 855 world cells are present for every integer m=16,...,300, and every preferred ground passed the numerical resolution checks. Zeta is globally even in all 285 cases; DH has 184 even and 101 odd global winners; the planted world has 146 even and 139 odd winners. The smallest empirical global-gap/error ratio is 4.1083×10⁵⁹. The maximum stored unit-norm-squared deviation is 4.6717×10⁻¹¹⁸. Exactly 110 LOW zeta blocks used shifted refinement, with 110 explicitly null LOW second pairs and zero native fallbacks. `FINAL_CHECKS.json`, `completeness.json`, `spectra.csv` and the PNG/SVG plots record the completed run.
